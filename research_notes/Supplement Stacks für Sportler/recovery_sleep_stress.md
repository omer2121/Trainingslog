> Evidence grades (A–D) and verdicts are my own inferences and appear only in the "Inferences" sections. The scale:
> - **A**: consistent meta-analytic RCT evidence of a meaningful effect in relevant (healthy or athletic) populations.
> - **B**: meta-analytic support, but with small or heterogeneous effects, clinical populations only, or evidence dominated by industry.
> - **C**: few, small or inconsistent trials, or surrogate markers only.
> - **D**: no meaningful human evidence, and/or the risk outweighs the benefit.

## 1. Sleep: magnesium (forms, non-deficient people), glycine, melatonin (doses; German legal status), L-theanine, tart cherry, apigenin, ashwagandha. How strong is the evidence?

### Takeaway
For healthy trainees, sleep supplements have weak-to-moderate evidence, and the effects are small and mostly subjective.
- **Melatonin** is the only one of these with authorised EU health claims *related to sleep* (1 mg for sleep onset; jet lag). "Only one" is my inference: I found no sleep claims for the others. In Germany it sits in a legal grey zone. Federal authorities treat it as a medicine regardless of dose, yet 1 mg food supplements are sold under split case law. Since **22 May 2026**, 3 mg for jet lag is an **OTC pharmacy medicine**, and 2 mg prolonged-release stays prescription-only.
- **L-theanine** improved subjective sleep outcomes in a 2025 meta-analysis.
- **Magnesium bisglycinate** (250 mg) had only a small effect (d = 0.2) in a 2025 RCT, and the effect was larger in people with low magnesium intake.
- **Glycine** and **tart cherry** have weak evidence, and isolated **apigenin** has not been tested.
- **Ashwagandha** has a moderate sleep effect (SMD −0.59), but it carries serious safety and regulatory problems (see Q2).

### Cited Findings

#### Magnesium
- **2025 RCT** (*Nature and Science of Sleep*, vol. 17). 155 adults aged 18–65 with self-reported poor sleep (not screened for deficiency) got magnesium bisglycinate (250 mg elemental Mg/day) or placebo.
  - Insomnia Severity Index change to week 4: −3.9 (95% CI −5.8 to −2.0) vs −2.3 (−4.1 to −0.4), p = 0.049.
  - The effect was small (Cohen's d = 0.2).
  - Exploratory analyses showed larger improvements in people with lower baseline dietary magnesium intake ("high responders").
  - Sources: [Tandfonline full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2147/NSS.S524348); [PubMed 40918053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40918053/); [Dove Press](https://www.dovepress.com/magnesium-bisglycinate-supplementation-in-healthy-adults-reporting--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NSS)
- EFSA has published a novel-food safety opinion on **magnesium L-threonate**, which also covers the bioavailability of Mg from it [T] — [EFSA Journal via PMC10934248](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10934248/)
- The German upper limit for supplemental magnesium (BfR, Dec 2017: ≤250 mg/day, split over ≥2 doses) is covered in Q4.

#### Glycine
- **Yamadera et al. 2007** (*Sleep and Biological Rhythms*): randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled crossover with about 11 volunteers.
  - 3 g glycine before bedtime improved subjective sleep quality and sleep efficacy.
  - It shortened polysomnographic latency to sleep onset and to slow-wave sleep.
  - It reduced daytime sleepiness and improved memory-recognition tasks.
  - Source: [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1479-8425.2007.00262.x)
- A **2024 systematic review** reportedly found that the glycine sleep studies are small (7–15 participants analysed, 2–4 nights each) and at high risk of bias. It concluded that the evidence is not strong enough to call glycine an established insomnia remedy. 3 g taken 30–60 min before bed is the most-studied regimen. [2°] — [The Hippie Scientist evidence review](https://thehippiescientist.net/guides/sleep/glycine-for-sleep/)

#### Melatonin: efficacy and safety signal
- **AHA Scientific Sessions, Nov 2025 abstract** (TriNetX electronic health records):
  - 130,828 adults with insomnia (mean age 56; 61% women). Users with ≥1 year of melatonin use were propensity-matched to non-users on 40 variables.
  - Over 5 years, incident heart failure was 4.6% (3,021) vs 2.7% (1,797), about 90% higher. HF hospitalisations were also higher, and all-cause mortality doubled.
  - It is observational, was not peer-reviewed at presentation, and cannot show causation.
  - Sources: [AHA newsroom](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/long-term-use-of-melatonin-supplements-to-support-sleep-may-have-negative-health-effects); [ACC, 3 Nov 2025](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2025/11/03/16/19/mon-melatonin-aha-2025); [Circulation abstract 4371606](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4371606); [ScienceDaily, Nov 2025](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/11/251104012959.htm)
- The US supplement trade group CRN publicly disputed the study. ScienceDaily ran another item on the finding on 29 Aug 2026 ("Long-term melatonin use linked to 90% higher heart failure risk") [T] — [CRN statement](https://www.crnusa.org/newsroom/crn-responds-melatonin-study-presented-aha-scientific-sessions-2025); [ScienceDaily, Aug 2026](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260829035228.htm)

#### Melatonin: legal status in Germany (which doses are what)
- **Federal authorities' position.** BfArM and BVL (and earlier BgVV) regard melatonin as pharmacologically active even at low doses, and they classify it as a medicine that needs authorisation *regardless of dose* ("dosisunabhängig") — [BfR: "Melatonin als Arzneimittel zulassungspflichtig – Empfehlung der Bundesinstitute erfolgt dosisunabhängig"](https://www.bfr.bund.de/veroeffentlichung/melatonin-als-arzneimittel-zulassungspflichtig-empfehlung-der-bundesinstitute-erfolgt-dosisunabhaengig/); [Deutsches Ärzteblatt: "BfArM: Melatonin ist ein Arzneimittel"](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/archiv/bfarm-melatonin-ist-ein-arzneimittel-ba9f3c2c-f0f7-443c-9bb6-f31d5fbfb846)
- **BfArM decision.** Products with a daily dose of **0.5–5 mg** are generally medicines that need authorisation. BfArM classified melatonin products as medicines "without restriction" in **Sept 2011**. [A?] — [APOTHEKE ADHOC](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/apothekenpraxis/melatonin-rechtslage-ungewiss-arzneimittel-oder-lebensmittel/); [DAZ, 13 Apr 2022](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2022/04/13/melatonin-als-nem-wo-sind-die-gesetzlichen-grenzen)
- **Courts are split.**
  - The Higher Administrative Court of North Rhine-Westphalia (OVG NRW) ruled on **29 Oct 2021** that "melatonin capsules are not generally medicines".
  - Case law is described as "very inconsistent".
  - Enforcement sits with the Länder food-control authorities, not with BVL or BfArM.
  - Sources: [OVG NRW press release](https://www.ovg.nrw.de/behoerde/presse/pressemitteilungen/01_archiv/2021/64_211029/index.php); [BVL FAQ on melatonin court cases](https://www.bvl.bund.de/SharedDocs/FAQ/DE/02_Unternehmer/01_Lebensmittel/05_FAQ_Melatonin/01_FAQ_Melatonin_Gericht.html); [DAZ 2022](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2022/04/13/melatonin-als-nem-wo-sind-die-gesetzlichen-grenzen)
- **Food supplements (NEM).** In practice they are sold at **1 mg/day** and may carry one of the two EU health claims: "trägt zur Linderung der subjektiven Jetlag-Empfindung bei" or "trägt dazu bei, die Einschlafzeit zu verkürzen". The condition is that 1 mg/day is not exceeded. — [PTAheute, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2022/04/21/wie-viel-melatonin-in-nem-ist-erlaubt); [DAZ 2022](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2022/04/13/melatonin-als-nem-wo-sind-die-gesetzlichen-grenzen)
- **New in 2026: OTC jet-lag medicine.**
  - From **22 May 2026**, the prescription requirement no longer applies to melatonin medicines with **3 mg per dosage unit, max 3 mg/day, pack size ≤30 mg**, taken orally for **jet lag in adults**.
  - **Melatal (Vitabalans)** is the first licensed OTC melatonin medicine (3 mg tablets, short-term jet-lag treatment).
  - Sources: [DAZ, 28 May 2026](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2026/05/28/jetzt-rezeptfrei-melatonin-bei-jetlag-und-aciclovir-buccaltabletten); [KVWL InVo, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.kvwl.de/fileadmin/user_upload/pdf/Mitglieder/Verordnung/Arzneimittel/Arzneimittelinformationen/InVo/InVo_Melatonin_AEnderung_Verschreibungspflicht_02.06.2026.pdf); [APOTHEKE ADHOC](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pharmazie/melatonin-3-mg-ist-rezeptfrei/); [PTA IN LOVE](https://www.pta-in-love.de/melatonin-otc-gegen-jetlag/)
- **Still prescription-only:**
  - 2 mg prolonged-release for short-term insomnia treatment in adults **≥55 years** (Circadin).
  - Paediatric products for ages 2–18 with autism spectrum disorder or Smith-Magenis syndrome.
  - Sources: [PTAheute, 21 Mar 2025](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2025/03/21/arzneimittel-mit-melatonin-welche-gibt-es); [EMA Circadin EPAR summary (DE)](https://www.ema.europa.eu/de/documents/overview/circadin-epar-summary-public_de.pdf)

#### L-theanine (sleep)
- **2025 SR/MA** (*Sleep Medicine Reviews*; Bond University group):
  - 19 articles and 897 participants (18 studies in the meta-analysis), searched to Sept 2024.
  - Doses ranged from 50 to 1,000 mg/day, from a single dose up to 8 weeks.
  - L-theanine significantly improved subjective sleep-onset latency, subjective daytime dysfunction and the overall subjective sleep-quality score. Effect sizes were not retrieved.
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079225000292); [Bond University portal](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/the-effects-of-l-theanine-consumption-on-sleep-outcomes-a-systema/); [NutraIngredients, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/03/31/review-explores-l-theanine-as-sleep-aid/)
- A further 2025 systematic review (*Nutritional Neuroscience*) and a 2026 review (*Frontiers in Nutrition*) exist [T] — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1028415X.2025.2556925); [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1922819/full)
- EU legal status (a novel-food problem) is covered in Q4.

#### Tart cherry (sleep)
- **Stretton et al. 2023 SR/MA** (*Current Sleep Medicine Reports* 9:225–233):
  - Subjectively recalled sleep efficiency and total sleep time did not improve significantly.
  - Objective sleep efficiency was higher with cherry than placebo (effect size 0.63).
  - Objective total sleep time improved, but heterogeneity was very high (I² = 81.5%, from a secondary summary).
  - Sources: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372285864_Too_Sour_to_be_True_Tart_Cherries_Prunus_cerasus_and_Sleep_a_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-analysis); [DNB PDF](https://d-nb.info/1308719787/34); I² figure [2°]: [Smarter Supplement Reviews](https://smartersupplementreviews.com/sleep/tart-cherry-for-sleep/)
- **Barforoush et al. 2025 SR** (*Food Science & Nutrition*): 7 interventional studies, of which 3 reported significant improvements in sleep duration, efficiency or onset. Evidence described as "limited and heterogeneous". — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/fsn3.70923)
- An RCT in elite female field-hockey players (2022) tested Montmorency juice on sleep after intermittent exercise [T] — [PMC9408103](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9408103/)

#### Apigenin / chamomile
- **2024 MA** (*Complementary Therapies in Medicine*): 10 chamomile trials, 772 participants. Chamomile extract improved subjective sleep quality and reduced night-time awakenings. [A?] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0965229924000591)
- **Hieu et al. 2019 SR/MA** (*Phytotherapy Research*; chamomile for anxiety, GAD, insomnia and sleep quality): chamomile improved sleep quality ("12 RCTs" per search summary). [A?] — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ptr.6349)
- **Insomnia specifically:** only one RCT exists (standardized chamomile extract pilot, chronic primary insomnia). It found no significant change in ISI, sleep duration, sleep efficiency or daytime functioning. [A?] — [PMC3198755](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3198755/)
- **Isolated apigenin** has not been tested for sleep in clinical trials. Chamomile extract contains about 0.8–1.2% apigenin among dozens of other bioactives. [2°, A?] — [Smarter Supplement Reviews: "Apigenin for Sleep: The Supplement Nobody Has Tested"](https://smartersupplementreviews.com/sleep/apigenin-for-sleep/)

#### Ashwagandha (sleep)
- **Cheah et al. 2021 SR/MA** (*PLOS One*):
  - 5 RCTs, 400 participants.
  - A small but significant effect on overall sleep: **SMD −0.59 (95% CI −0.75 to −0.42)**.
  - The effect was stronger in adults with **insomnia**, at **≥600 mg/day** and with **≥8 weeks** of use.
  - Mental alertness on rising and anxiety also improved. There was no effect on quality of life and no serious adverse events.
  - Source: [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0257843)

### Inferences
- **Grades for sleep** (my judgement; healthy trainees):

| Supplement | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Melatonin | B | Sleep onset and jet lag; authorised EU claims; best used for circadian shifts |
| L-theanine | B− | Subjective outcomes only; 2025 MA |
| Magnesium bisglycinate | C+ | Single 2025 RCT, d = 0.2, likely limited to low-intake people |
| Ashwagandha | B− for effect | But not recommended because of the Q2 safety and regulatory picture |
| Tart cherry | C | Objective efficiency only, heterogeneous |
| Chamomile | C | Subjective quality; null in insomnia |
| Glycine | C | Tiny crossovers, high risk of bias; but cheap and low risk at 3 g |
| Isolated apigenin | D | Untested |

- **Healthy good sleepers:** because almost all positive data come from poor sleepers or insomnia populations, a trainee who already sleeps well should expect little to no benefit.
- **Melatonin, practical legal reading for Germany in 2026:**
  - The clean, legal route is the 3 mg jet-lag OTC medicine from pharmacies (short-term use only).
  - 1 mg food supplements are still sold, but their status is disputed by federal authorities.
  - Anything above 1 mg for insomnia is a prescription medicine (2 mg prolonged-release, ≥55 years).
- **Melatonin and the HF signal:** nightly long-term use is not supported. The heart-failure signal is probably confounded by indication, because sicker patients are more likely to have melatonin recorded in their EHR. Also, OTC use in the US is often not documented at all, so misclassification is plausible. This is my reasoning, not a sourced critique. Still, it argues against habitual multi-year use.
- **Magnesium:** the 2025 RCT suggests that any benefit lives mainly in people with low dietary magnesium. It is worth trying only if intake is low, and staying at or below the 250 mg/day German/EU supplemental ceiling (see Q4).

### Gaps
- **Melatonin effect sizes:** no melatonin meta-analysis numbers were retrieved (e.g., minutes of sleep-onset latency saved, dose-response). Background knowledge, not verified here: Ferracioli-Oda 2013 reported about −7 min sleep-onset latency and +8 min total sleep time.
- **EU melatonin claim conditions** under Reg. (EU) 432/2012 (1 mg close to bedtime for sleep onset; ≥0.5 mg for jet lag, from background knowledge) were not verified this session. German sources say NEM stay at ≤1 mg/day.
- **AHA melatonin study:** whether it has been peer-reviewed and published by Aug 2026 (the ScienceDaily item hints at later coverage) was not verified.
- **Magnesium forms:** no head-to-head evidence on forms (oxide, citrate, glycinate, L-threonate) for sleep was retrieved. L-threonate sleep RCTs and its EU novel-food authorisation status were not verified. Magnesium for cramps was not researched.
- **Glycine:** the primary 2024 systematic review behind the "high risk of bias" statement was not identified.
- **L-theanine and tart cherry:** L-theanine effect sizes and the tart-cherry dosing regimens used in sleep trials were not retrieved.

## 2. Stress, anxiety and mood: ashwagandha (extracts, effect sizes, safety, EU and BfR regulation, 2026 status), rhodiola, L-theanine, saffron, omega-3 (EPA), lavender oil (Silexan), St John's wort, 5-HTP

### Takeaway
- **Best evidence, lowest risk:**
  - **Silexan 80 mg/day**, a lavender-oil medicine. RCTs and meta-analyses support it, and a network meta-analysis found 80 mg equal to paroxetine.
  - **Saffron about 30 mg/day.** In an 8-trial meta-analysis (2024) it was no different from SSRIs.
  - **EPA-dominant omega-3 (≥60% EPA, ~≤1 g EPA/day)**, but only as an adjunct in depression.
- **Ashwagandha** shows large but heterogeneous effects on stress, anxiety and cortisol, and a 2025 meta-analysis found **no effect on perceived stress**. It also carries **liver-injury, thyroid, pregnancy and drug-interaction concerns**. The regulatory picture:
  - BfR warned in **Sept 2024**.
  - The EU Heads of Agencies recommended an **Article 8 procedure** (June 2024), which could lead to an EU-wide ban. It was actively debated in Brussels in **July 2026**.
  - **Denmark** banned it (2023), and the **Netherlands** intends to (2026).
  - A German **Verbraucherzentrale market check (Apr 2026)** found most products far above a ~10 mg/day withanolide reference level and missing warnings.
- **Rhodiola** evidence is weak and mixed.
- **St John's wort** works for mild-to-moderate depression, but it is a medicine with major interactions (e.g., the pill, ciclosporin, anticoagulants).
- **5-HTP** carries a serotonin-syndrome risk with serotonergic drugs and a historical EMS/contaminant concern, so avoid it.

### Cited Findings

#### Ashwagandha: efficacy (stress, anxiety, cortisol)
- **Akhgarjand et al. 2022 SR/MA**: vs placebo, anxiety **SMD −1.55 (95% CI −2.37 to −0.74)** and stress **SMD −1.75 (−2.29 to −1.22)**. [A?] — [PubMed 36017529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36017529/)
- **2024 SR/MA** (*EXPLORE*): 9 RCTs, 558 patients. Significant effects on the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS), the Hamilton Anxiety Scale and serum cortisol vs placebo. — [PubMed 39348746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39348746/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1550830724001691)
- **Albalawi 2025 SR/MA** (*Nutrition and Health*): RCTs from Jan 2012 to Feb 2024; 7 studies on cortisol and 6 on perceived stress (488 participants). Result: **"significant cortisol reduction but no effects on perceived stress"**. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/02601060251363647)
- **2025 SR/MA** (*BJPsych Open*): searched to 8 Sept 2024; 15 studies, 873 patients. Significant reductions in cortisol, PSS and HAM-A. [A?] — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/effects-of-ashwagandha-supplements-on-cortisol-stress-and-anxiety-levels-in-adults-a-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis/6F2D7847C1F64707F2034A45FD6CF0C0); [PMC12242034](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12242034/)
- A **2026 systematic review with dose-response meta-analysis** of ashwagandha and mental health in adults exists (*Complementary Therapies in Medicine*); results were not retrieved [T] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0965229926000087)
- **Typical marketed dosing:** 300–600 mg/day of extracts standardized to 5–10% withanolides, which is about 15–60 mg withanolides/day. Supplement doses on the market range from 225 to 1,350 mg/day. [2°, industry blogs] — [Nordic Oil](https://www.nordicoil.de/blogs/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/ashwagandha-dosierung); [optimum-performance](https://optimum-performance.de/tools/blog/ashwagandha-withanolide-warum-die-dosierung-alles-entscheidet); [DocCheck Flexikon](https://flexikon.doccheck.com/de/Ashwagandha) [A?]

#### Ashwagandha: safety signals
- **BfR communication 039/2024 (10 Sept 2024).**
  - Ashwagandha ("Schlafbeere") preparations can affect blood sugar, sex hormones, the central nervous system, the thyroid, adrenal and liver function, and the immune and endocrine systems (cortisol, blood sugar, thyroid and sex hormones).
  - Case reports of liver damage, including **acute liver failure**, "give particular reason for caution".
  - It is **not suitable for children, pregnant or breastfeeding women, or people with existing or previous liver disease**.
  - There are indications of **interactions with antidiabetics, antihypertensives and immunosuppressants**.
  - Sources: [BfR Mitteilung 039/2024 (PDF)](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/343/ashwagandha-schlafbeeren-praeparate-mit-moeglichen-gesundheitsrisiken.pdf); [Deutsches Ärzteblatt](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/news/bundesinstitut-warnt-vor-schlafbeerenpraeparaten-a1b94183-6499-48bb-a0ef-bfbf490252fb); [Gelbe Liste](https://www.gelbe-liste.de/apotheke/sicherheitsbedenken-bfr-ashwagandha-schlafbeere); [DAS PTA MAGAZIN](https://www.das-pta-magazin.de/news/ashwagandha-nichts-fuer-leberkranke-3553195.html)
  - One search summary described BfR as advising against ashwagandha supplements *overall*. Other summaries only name the risk groups, so check the exact wording in the PDF. [A?]
- **Other side effects** reported: nausea, drowsiness, headache, dizziness and skin reactions — [Verbraucherzentrale market check 2026](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/sites/default/files/2026-04/bericht-mc-ashwagandha_2026.pdf); [VZ NRW consumer page](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.nrw/wissen/lebensmittel/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/ashwagandha-welche-wirkung-hat-es-und-wo-koennen-gefahren-liegen-117720)
- **Pregnancy:** the Verbraucherzentrale explicitly warns of **miscarriage** risk [T] — [APOTHEKE ADHOC](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pta-live/ashwagandha-verbraucherzentrale-warnt-vor-fehlgeburten/)
- **Systematic literature review, 2026** (conference-level, HCA Healthcare North Texas; Teplitsky, Sebaugh et al.):
  - **37 cases** of ashwagandha-associated liver injury; mean age 41.
  - 78% presented with jaundice and 51% with pruritus; the pattern was mostly hepatocellular.
  - Most cases resolved, but **several ended in death or liver transplantation**.
  - Source: [HCA Healthcare Scholarly Commons](https://scholarlycommons.hcahealthcare.com/northtexas2026/34/)
- **Indian case series:** 23 patients (2019–2022), 8 described in detail. Cholestatic hepatitis was most common, and 3 patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure died. [2°] — [Dr Kumar Discovery summary](https://drkumardiscovery.com/posts/ashwagandha-induced-liver-injurya-case-series-from-india-literature/)
- **Latency** of hepatotoxicity ranged from **3 weeks to 10 months**, with resolution after stopping; liver-function monitoring is advised during use. [A?] — [LiverTox (NIH)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/); [Cureus case report 2024](https://www.cureus.com/articles/263978-ashwagandha-induced-hepatic-injury-a-case-report)
- **2025 critical review** (*Phytotherapy Research*): reported adverse effects include hepatotoxicity, **thyroid dysfunction** and **pharmacokinetic drug interactions**. Preclinical work shows increased thyroid activity and metabolism. — [Wiley ptr.70158](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.70158); [PMC13436050](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13436050/); see also "Is It Safe? Part 2: preclinical review" [T] — [Wiley ptr.70090](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.70090)

#### Ashwagandha: regulatory status in the EU and Germany (2022 to Sept 2026)
- **Sweden, Sept 2022:** the Swedish Food Agency said the Danish risk assessment could be applied in Sweden, with municipalities deciding case by case. — [Vitafoods Insights](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/regulations/eu-regulatory-update-on-ashwagandha)
- **Denmark:** banned ashwagandha in food supplements in **2023**, based on a risk assessment by DTU (Technical University of Denmark). — [Vitafoods Insights](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/regulations/eu-regulatory-update-on-ashwagandha)
  - Chronology note: Sweden referred to "the Danish risk assessment" in Sept 2022, so the DTU assessment already existed then. The same source dates the formal Danish ban to 2023.
- **EU Heads of Food Safety Agencies (HoA) working group, June 2024.**
  - It rated ashwagandha **"high priority"** and recommended prioritising it for an **Article 8 procedure under Reg. (EC) 1925/2006** "due to (potential) effects on reproduction, thyroid hormones, acetylcholinesterase, the immune system and due to liver toxicity".
  - An Article 8 procedure can end in restrictions, labelling requirements or an EU-wide ban in food supplements.
  - EFSA would assess safety, but the timeline is unknown.

#### Saffron

[A?] — [Phytotherapy Research 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.8110)

#### Omega-3 (EPA) for mood
- **Liao et al. 2019 MA** (*Translational Psychiatry*):

- The **ISNPR** (International Society for Nutritional Psychiatry Research) clinical guideline recommends EPA-predominant omega-3 for major depressive disorder (MDD). [A?] — [via Liao 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-019-0515-5)
- Another analysis linked **≥60% EPA and EPA doses of ≥1 to <2 g/day** with significant reductions in depression severity. The attribution is unclear. [A?] — [Pharmacological Research 2025 review on EPA in treatment-resistant depression](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1043661825002828); [PLEFA 2023 MA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952327823000418)

#### Lavender oil (Silexan)
- **Yap et al. 2019 network MA** (*Scientific Reports*): 5 studies; 524 participants on Silexan **80 mg** and 121 on 160 mg; Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAMA) outcome. **The 80 mg effect was the same as paroxetine.** — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-54529-9)
- **Kasper et al. MA in subthreshold anxiety** (PubMed 29150713, online 2017): Silexan beat placebo on HAMA over 10 weeks, a mean difference of **3.83 points (95% CI 1.28 to 6.37)**. — [PubMed 29150713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29150713/); [PMC6726711](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6726711/)
- **Kasper et al. 2014 RCT in generalized anxiety disorder** (*IJNP* 17(6):859): HAMA fell by **12.8 ± 8.7** on 80 mg/day vs **9.5 ± 9.0** on placebo. Both 160 mg and 80 mg beat placebo (p < 0.01); paroxetine was the comparator. — [IJNP](https://academic.oup.com/ijnp/article/17/6/859/691858)
- A **2022 MA** across anxiety disorders (*Eur Arch Psychiatry Clin Neurosci*) exists [T] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00406-022-01547-w)

#### St John's wort (Johanniskraut)
- **Germany:** St John's wort medicines for **moderate depression are prescription-only**. Higher-dosed, pharmaceutical-quality preparations come from pharmacies. It is credited with benefit in mild-to-moderate depression. [A?] — [TK](https://www.tk.de/techniker/krankheit-und-behandlungen/erkrankungen/behandlungen-und-medizin/psychische-gesundheit/depression-burnout/kann-johanniskraut-bei-depressionen-helfen-2016412); [AOK](https://www.aok.de/pk/magazin/koerper-psyche/psychologie/johanniskraut-so-wirkt-die-heilpflanze-gegen-depressionen/)

#### 5-HTP
- The most common adverse effects are **gastrointestinal** (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea) and dose-dependent. [A?] — [Poison Control](https://www.poison.org/articles/5htp-safety-concerns-173)

People on **SSRIs, MAOIs, tricyclics or other serotonergic drugs** should not take 5-HTP without medical supervision. The clinical literature on actual cases is limited. — [MSKCC](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/5-htp-01); [OPSS (US DoD)](https://www.opss.org/article/5-htp-depression-and-other-conditions)

Some 5-HTP users have developed EMS, and it is unclear whether 5-HTP, a contaminant or another factor is responsible. — [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/natural/794.html); [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/vitamins/ai/ingredientmono-794/5-htp)

- **Ashwagandha for a German trainee in 2026: not recommended as a routine supplement.**
  - The safety signals (liver injury with deaths and transplants, thyroid, pregnancy, drug interactions) are unresolved.
  - An EU process that could end in a ban is live.
  - Marketed products are often dosed far above the reference level the Verbraucherzentrale used.
- **Dose arithmetic (my calculation):** clinical-style doses such as 600 mg of a 5% extract give about 30 mg withanolides/day, already about 3 times the ~10 mg/day reference implied by the Verbraucherzentrale.
- **If a user still insists on ashwagandha:**
  - Choose a root-only product with declared withanolide content.
  - Use it short-term only. Trials typically run ≤8–12 weeks (background knowledge; the Cheah subgroup was ≥8 weeks), and I found no long-term safety data.
  - Do not use it with liver disease, thyroid disease or medication, pregnancy or trying to conceive, or with antidiabetics, antihypertensives or immunosuppressants.
  - Stop at once if jaundice, itching, dark urine or unusual fatigue appear.
  - Cycling does **not** make it safe: liver-injury latency ran from 3 weeks to 10 months.
- **India's leaf ban** (Apr 2026) could affect leaf-containing branded extracts. Whether a specific brand contains leaf needs checking (see Gaps).
- **Low mood or anxiety:** the most defensible options are the licensed medicine Silexan (pharmacy) and saffron (food supplement). Anyone with a diagnosed depression or anxiety disorder should use these under medical care rather than self-treat.
- **St John's wort:** do not stack it with anything serotonergic, or with the pill or any regular medication. In Germany, the effective doses are pharmacy medicines anyway.

## 3. Recovery and soreness: omega-3, tart cherry, curcumin, collagen plus vitamin C, creatine and protein, and whether high-dose antioxidants (vitamins C/E) blunt adaptations

### Takeaway
- **Foundations for a hypertrophy trainee:** adequate daily protein, optionally with about 40 g casein before sleep, plus creatine. Creatine's core evidence was not re-reviewed here; new 2024–2026 data show single high doses reduce cognitive decline during sleep deprivation.
- **Omega-3 and curcumin** moderately reduce soreness (DOMS) and creatine kinase (CK), with SMDs of about −0.4 to −0.75.
- **Tart cherry** mainly speeds strength (MVC) recovery and lowers CRP, with low-to-moderate certainty.
- **Collagen plus vitamin C** (15 g about 1 h before loading) is promising for tendons.
- The Australian Institute of Sport (AIS) lists all four in **Group B** (emerging evidence).
- **Chronic high-dose vitamin C/E** does not improve strength or hypertrophy and may blunt it, so avoid it around training.

### Cited Findings

#### Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)
- **Meta-analysis of 41 RCTs** (2011–2025) on EPA+DHA in sport: IL-6, TNF-α, CK and DOMS were moderately reduced, with **SMDs of −0.4 to −0.7**. [A?] — [PubMed 41891174 "Effects of Omega-3 Supplementation on Inflammation and Recovery in Sports: A Meta-Analysis"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41891174/)
- **Meta-analysis of 9 placebo-controlled eccentric-exercise trials:** DOMS **Hedges' g −0.75 (95% CI −1.14 to −0.36)** and CK **g −0.40 (−0.70 to −0.10)**. Methodological limits prevented any assessment of effective dosing. [A?: the source is either the 2026 MA (PubMed 42124047) or the earlier eccentric-exercise MA (PMC7195643)] — [PubMed 42124047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42124047/); [PMC7195643](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7195643/)
- **2024 RCT** (overweight or obese men, cycling HIIT): 4 weeks of omega-3 blunted the rise in CK, reduced calf pain and sped up leg-strength recovery. — [PubMed 38841630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38841630/)
- A systematic review in military service members (performance, recovery, injury) exists [T] — [PMC11768049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11768049/)

#### Tart cherry (recovery)
- **2026 SR/MA** (*Sports Medicine – Open*):
  - Tart-cherry juice significantly improved recovery of **maximal voluntary contraction (MVC)** at several time points, increasingly so between **48 and 96 h**.
  - It reduced **CRP** from immediately after exercise up to 48 h.
  - **No significant pooled effects** were found for soreness, CK, IL-6, TNF-α or range of motion at most time points.
  - Certainty of evidence: **low to moderate** (heterogeneity, imprecision).
  - Sources: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-026-00993-3); [PubMed 41945263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41945263/); [PMC13057130](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13057130/)
- **Hill et al. 2021 SR/MA** (*IJSNEM*): a small beneficial effect on muscle soreness. [A?] — [Human Kinetics](https://journals.humankinetics.com/abstract/journals/ijsnem/31/2/article-p154.xml)
- A 2026 narrative review with an applied monitoring framework [T] — [PubMed 41956499](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41956499/)

#### Curcumin
- **2024 MA** (*PLOS One*): effect on muscle soreness **−0.61**; CK **−137.32 U/L**; IL-6 and range of motion also improved. — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0299135); [PubMed 39008500](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39008500/)
- **2022 SR with dose-response MA** (*Phytotherapy Research*): serum CK −65.98 IU/L and soreness reduced [A?]. There was a significant **non-linear dose-response for TNF-α**. — [Wiley ptr.7477](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.7477)
- **2021 MA** (*Bulletin of the National Research Centre*): soreness at 4 days post-exercise was 1.24 points lower than placebo. [A?] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42269-021-00664-1)
- **Doses** in trials were mostly **150–1,500 mg/day** curcumin, often in enhanced-bioavailability forms (piperine, other formulations). — [search summary incl. JISSN 2024 SR](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15502783.2024.2434217) [A?]

#### Collagen plus vitamin C (tendons and ligaments)
- **Shaw et al. 2017:** **15 g vitamin C-enriched gelatin taken 1 h before** a short bout of jumping **doubled** the blood marker of collagen synthesis (amino-terminal propeptide of type I collagen) vs placebo; 5 g had less effect. — [ResearchGate (paper)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/310471896_Vitamin_C-enriched_gelatin_supplementation_before_intermittent_activity_augments_collagen_synthesis)
- **2025/26 systematic review** "Collagen Supplementation on Tendon-Related Structural and Performance Outcomes" (searched to May 2025):
  - It reportedly found **8 high-quality RCTs** and **strong evidence (described as "GRADE A")** for increased **tendon cross-sectional area and stiffness** with **15–30 g/day** combined with resistance training. [2°/A?: the GRADE wording may come from blog summaries]
  - Sources: [PMC13028264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13028264/); [Move Physiotherapy](https://www.movephysiotherapy.com.au/blog/collagen-supplementation-tendon-injuries); [FitCraft](https://getfitcraft.com/science/collagen-supplementation-tendon-research)
- **Mechanism and timing:** collagen peptides are rich in glycine, proline, hydroxyproline and hydroxylysine. Vitamin C is the cofactor for hydroxylating proline and lysine. Blood amino acids peak about **60 min** after ingestion, so they are available while loading stimulates collagen synthesis. — [PMC13028264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13028264/) [A?]
- An earlier SR of collagen peptides (body composition, collagen synthesis, recovery from joint injury and exercise) exists [T] — [PMC8521576](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8521576/)

#### Creatine
- **Gordji-Nejad et al. 2024** (*Scientific Reports*): a **single high dose of creatine monohydrate (0.35 g/kg)** during sleep deprivation changed PCr/Pi and ATP, **prevented a drop in brain pH**, and **improved cognitive performance and processing speed**. Effects appeared within 3–4 h. — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9); [PMC10902318](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10902318/)
  - Popular reports describe about 24.5% faster processing speed and 10–16% better short-term memory and logic. [2°] — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/sleep/creatine-for-sleep-deprivation)
- **2026 study** (*Nutrients* 18(8):1192): a single dose of creatine lessened the sleep-deprivation decline in logical and numerical tasks, language-processing speed and the Psychomotor Vigilance Test. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/8/1192)
- **Depression:** adding creatine to cognitive behavioural therapy improved outcomes beyond therapy alone. The primary trial was not retrieved. [A?] — [JPBS review "Creatine Supplementation: More Is Likely Better for Brain Bioenergetics"](https://jpbs.hapres.com/htmls/JPBS_1766_Detail.html)

#### Protein (including pre-sleep)
- **Pre-sleep casein:**
  - **20–40 g about 30 min before sleep** increases overnight protein synthesis in young men, and **at least ~40 g** is needed for a robust rise in muscle protein synthesis (MPS).
  - Overnight MPS is about **22–33% higher** with ~40 g casein than with an isocaloric carbohydrate drink. [A?]
  - Sources: [GSSI Sports Science Exchange 117](https://www.gssiweb.org/sports-science-exchange/article/sse-117-protein-ingestion-prior-to-sleep-potential-for-optimizing-post-exercise-recovery); [Trommelen et al., AJP-Endo 2016](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpendo.00273.2016); [PAN journal review](https://www.e-pan.org/journal/view.php?number=232)
- **Long-term:** pre-sleep protein **can augment gains in muscle fibre size, strength and mass over 10–12 weeks** of resistance training in **young** men, but not in elderly men. [A?] — [Nutrients 2016 review](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/8/12/763)
- **Morning training:** pre-bed casein **did not improve acute functional recovery** when exercise was done in the morning (2019 RCT). [T] — [PMC6359469](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6359469/)
- **Endurance:** pre-sleep protein increased overnight **mitochondrial** protein synthesis after endurance exercise (2023 RCT). [T] — [Sports Medicine](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01822-3)
- A 2021 systematic review of pre-sleep protein and muscle outcomes exists (*JSAMS*); results were not retrieved [T] — [JSAMS](https://www.jsams.org/article/S1440-2440(20)30719-2/abstract)

#### High-dose antioxidants (vitamins C and E) and training adaptations
- **Dutra et al. 2020 SR/MA** (*Journal of Sports Medicine*): vitamins C and E had **no effect on force production** after chronic strength training. Most evidence suggests they do **not boost muscle growth and could attenuate hypertrophy over time**. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2020/3505209); [PMC6973181](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6973181/)
- **2019 SR/MA** on vitamins C and E and exercise-induced adaptations: **no effects on lean mass or strength** after resistance training. [A?] — [PubMed 31851538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31851538/)
- **Double-blind RCT (*Nutrition*, 2022/23):** vitamin C/E supplementation **may blunt upper-body strength and hypertrophy gains** in **trained young men**. — [PubMed 36283241](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36283241/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899900722002611)
- Practitioner review [2°] — [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/antioxidants/)

#### Classification by the Australian Institute of Sport (AIS)
- **Group A** = strong scientific evidence for use in sport (e.g., sports drinks, caffeine). **Group B** = emerging support that deserves more research, to be used under a research protocol or case-managed monitoring.
- **Fish oils, curcumin, collagen and tart cherry** (as a "food polyphenol") are all in **Group B**.
- Sources: [AIS/ASC Group B](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_b); [AIS framework PDF copy](https://www.gomotionapp.com/usanybgcnwm/UserFiles/Image/QuickUpload/ais-supplement-framework_026372.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Grades for recovery** (my judgement):

| Supplement | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Protein (total daily) | A | Background, not re-reviewed here |
| Pre-sleep casein | B | Robust overnight MPS; chronic benefit mainly in young men, likely partly from higher total protein |
| Creatine | A for training adaptations | Not re-reviewed here |
| Creatine | C+ for cognition under sleep loss | Very high single doses (0.35 g/kg ≈ 28 g for 80 kg) |
| Omega-3 | B | DOMS/CK |
| Curcumin | B− | DOMS/CK, small trials; liver caveat (Q4) |
| Tart cherry | B− | Strength recovery/CRP; not soreness |
| Collagen + vitamin C | B−/C | Tendon; small trials, mechanistic markers |
| High-dose vitamin C/E | D (harmful) | Chronic use alongside training |

- **Why reducing soreness is not the goal:** in hypertrophy training, soreness itself is not the target, and chronically dampening inflammation could in theory blunt adaptation, as shown for vitamin C/E. I found **no direct evidence** in this session that tart cherry, curcumin or omega-3 blunt hypertrophy. That concern is extrapolation.
- **Where recovery aids fit:** they make most sense in competition, in congested schedules or in unusual high-eccentric blocks, not as a daily habit.
- **Collagen plus vitamin C** is the one recovery product with a plausible, specific target (tendon and ligament loading and rehab). The Shaw protocol is 15 g gelatin plus vitamin C about 1 h before loading.

## 4. Doses, timing, cycling, side effects, contraindications and drug interactions (plus German/EU legal status)

### Takeaway
Hard limits and red flags:
- **Magnesium** from supplements: **≤250 mg/day, split over ≥2 doses** (BfR, based on the EU Scientific Committee on Food, SCF). Diarrhoea starts at about 360 mg.
- **Melatonin:** short-term only (the jet-lag OTC medicine is 3 mg, max 3 mg/day, pack ≤30 mg).
- **Omega-3:** EMA 2023 found a **dose-dependent atrial fibrillation (AF) risk** in people with cardiovascular disease or risk factors (roughly +12% at ~1 g/day and ~+50% at 1.8–4 g/day).
- **Turmeric/curcumin**, especially with piperine: **hepatocellular liver injury** linked to HLA-B\*35:01.
- **Ashwagandha:** liver, thyroid, pregnancy; interacts with antidiabetics, antihypertensives and immunosuppressants.
- **St John's wort:** enzyme induction, so the pill can fail and ciclosporin, anticoagulants and some antidepressants are affected.
- **5-HTP:** serotonin syndrome with SSRIs, MAOIs or tricyclics.
- **Synthetic L-theanine:** flagged as an **unauthorised novel food** in the EU (RASFF alert 2024; an authorisation application is pending).

### Cited Findings

#### Magnesium: upper limit (Germany/EU)
- **BfR (Dec 2017) recommendation:** food supplements should provide at most **250 mg magnesium/day**, **split over at least two intakes**.
  - The figure is based on the **EU Scientific Committee on Food (2001) upper limit of 250 mg/day for supplemental magnesium** (supplements plus fortified foods).
  - No diarrhoea was seen up to 250 mg/day, while about **360 mg/day** can cause mild diarrhoea.
  - It applies from age 4.
  - Sources: [BfR press release](https://www.bfr.bund.de/presseinformation/bfr-bewertet-tageshoechstmenge-fuer-magnesium-in-nahrungsergaenzungsmitteln/); [BfR opinion PDF (DOI 10.17590/20171212-074919)](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/343/bfr-bewertet-empfohlene-tageshoechstmenge-fuer-die-aufnahme-von-magnesium-ueber-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel.pdf); [DAZ, 15 Dec 2017](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2017/12/15/bfr-maximal-250-mg-magnesium-pro-tag); [Ernährungs Umschau 2018](https://www.ernaehrungs-umschau.de/print-news/15-01-2018-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-mehr-als-250-mg-magnesium-pro-tag-nicht-empfehlenswert/); [Lebensmittelklarheit](https://www.lebensmittelklarheit.de/fragen-antworten/wie-viel-magnesium-darf-einem-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-stecken); [APOTHEKE ADHOC](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pharmazie/magnesium-magnesium-bfr-stutzt-hoechstmenge-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/)

#### Melatonin: dose and legal classes (summary of Q1)
- **Food supplement (NEM):** ≤1 mg/day, with the EU claims for sleep onset and jet lag; the legal status is disputed by federal authorities.
- **OTC medicine (since 22 May 2026):** 3 mg tablets, **max 3 mg/day, pack ≤30 mg**, for **jet lag in adults**.
- **Prescription (Rx):** 2 mg prolonged-release for adults ≥55 (short-term insomnia), and paediatric products.
- Sources: [PTAheute 2022](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2022/04/21/wie-viel-melatonin-in-nem-ist-erlaubt); [DAZ, 28 May 2026](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2026/05/28/jetzt-rezeptfrei-melatonin-bei-jetlag-und-aciclovir-buccaltabletten); [PTAheute 2025](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2025/03/21/arzneimittel-mit-melatonin-welche-gibt-es)
- **Long-term safety signal:** ≥1 year of use was associated with about 90% higher heart-failure incidence over 5 years (observational AHA 2025 abstract; causation unproven) — [AHA newsroom](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/long-term-use-of-melatonin-supplements-to-support-sleep-may-have-negative-health-effects)

#### Omega-3: atrial fibrillation (AF)
- **EMA safety committee (PRAC), 2023:**
  - Reviewed systematic reviews and meta-analyses of large RCTs with more than **80,000 patients**, mostly with cardiovascular disease or risk factors.
  - Found a **dose-dependent increase in AF risk** with **omega-3-acid ethyl ester medicines** vs placebo, highest at **4 g/day**.
  - The product information (SmPC) was updated, and a Direct Healthcare Professional Communication (DHPC) letter was sent to doctors.
  - Sources: [EMA DHPC](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/dhpc/direct-healthcare-professional-communication-dhpc-omega-3-acid-ethyl-ester-medicines-dose-dependent-increased-risk-atrial-fibrillation-patients-established-cardiovascular-diseases-or-cardiovascular_en.pdf); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/997348); [AIFA note, 8 Nov 2023](https://www.aifa.gov.it/documents/20142/1804929/2023.11.08_NII_omega-3_EN.pdf)
- **Dose pattern:**
  - **~1,000 mg/day EPA+DHA ≈ +12% AF risk**; **1,800–4,000 mg/day ≈ +50%**. [A?]
  - Only studies in **high-CVD-risk patients on >1,500 mg/day** showed a significant increase (pooled OR 1.48). [A?]
  - Sources: [Review "Omega-3 and Risk of AF: vagally-mediated double-edged sword"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12122841/); [updated MA of 34 trials, 114,326 people (2025)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12723971/)

#### Turmeric/curcumin: liver injury
- **US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network (DILIN), 10 cases** (*Am J Med*, 2023):
  - Turmeric liver injury is usually **hepatocellular**, with a **latency of 1–4 months**, and is **strongly linked to HLA-B\*35:01**.
  - 7 patients carried the allele (2 homozygous); allele frequency was 0.450 vs 0.056–0.069 in population controls.
  - Sources: [Am J Med](https://www.amjmed.com/article/S0002-9343(22)00740-9/fulltext); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002934322007409)
- **Piperine:** it inhibits glucuronidation and can raise curcumin bioavailability **up to 2,000%**, which may raise liver risk; piperine plus high-dose curcumin may act together. A **2025 case report** described drug-induced liver injury in a 35-year-old man from a turmeric-plus-piperine supplement.
  - Sources: [Cureus/PMC12633785 case report](https://www.cureus.com/articles/405932-drug-induced-liver-injury-secondary-to-turmeric-supplement-containing-piperine-a-case-report); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/turmeric-black-pepper-supplements-linked-liver-injury); [ACG 2024 abstract](https://journals.lww.com/ajg/fulltext/2024/10001/s4537_turmeric_s_bitter_twist__drug_induced_liver.4538.aspx)

#### Ashwagandha: contraindications and interactions (summary of Q2)
- **Do not use** if you are a child, pregnant or breastfeeding, or have current or past liver disease.
- **Interactions** with antidiabetics, antihypertensives and immunosuppressants.
- **Possible effects** on thyroid, sex hormones, cortisol and the central nervous system (BfR, Sept 2024).
- Sources: [BfR Mitteilung 039/2024](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/343/ashwagandha-schlafbeeren-praeparate-mit-moeglichen-gesundheitsrisiken.pdf); [Deutsches Ärzteblatt](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/news/bundesinstitut-warnt-vor-schlafbeerenpraeparaten-a1b94183-6499-48bb-a0ef-bfbf490252fb)
- **Liver-injury latency:** 3 weeks to 10 months [A?] — [LiverTox](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/)

#### St John's wort and 5-HTP: interactions (summary of Q2)
- **St John's wort:** liver-enzyme induction can make the contraceptive pill fail and causes interactions with tricyclic antidepressants, anticoagulants, ciclosporin and other immunosuppressants, and some cancer drugs — [onmeda](https://www.onmeda.de/therapie/wirkstoffe/johanniskraut-id204038/); [APOTHEKE ADHOC](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pta-live/vorsicht-bei-johanniskraut-und-ciclosporin-repetitorium-antidepressiva/)
- **5-HTP:** avoid alongside SSRIs, MAOIs, tricyclics or other serotonergic drugs because of serotonin-syndrome risk; GI side effects are dose-dependent; there is a historical EMS/contaminant concern — [MSKCC](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/5-htp-01); [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/natural/794.html)

#### L-theanine: EU novel-food status
- **2024 application:** an applicant requested EU authorisation of L-theanine as a novel food under Reg. (EU) 2015/2283 for use in **food supplements** (EC summary of an ongoing application). — [European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a498e343-3403-427d-a95d-6a8fa4226c3b_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2024-15277.pdf)
- **2024 RASFF alert** 2024.4198: "Unauthorised novel food; L-theanine…". — [RASFF Window](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/rasff-window/screen/notification/688243)
- **Source matters:** L-theanine **extracted from green tea** is described as acceptable in food supplements, while L-theanine from other sources (e.g., synthetic) is **not approved**. [2°, UK manufacturer, Apr 2024] — [Vitamin Manufacture UK](https://www.vitaminmanufacture.co.uk/2024/04/what-are-novel-foods/)

- **Cycling:**
  - Ashwagandha: the evidence covers ≤8–12 weeks, and liver-injury latency extends to 10 months, so neither "cycling" nor short courses guarantee safety.
  - Melatonin: meant for short courses (jet lag).
  - Rhodiola: trials lasted 2–6 weeks.
  - For magnesium, glycine, theanine, collagen, creatine and protein, no evidence **requiring** cycling was found. That is an absence of evidence, not proof.
- **Stacking red flags:**
  - St John's wort or 5-HTP with any serotonergic drug (SSRIs/SNRIs, triptans, tramadol, MDMA). The drug examples beyond SSRIs/MAOIs/tricyclics are my inference from the serotonergic mechanism.
  - Ashwagandha together with other hepatotoxic botanicals, turmeric/piperine or alcohol (inference: additive liver risk).
  - Several magnesium-containing products that together exceed 250 mg/day.
- **Contamination and doping:** the risk for tested athletes was not researched (see Gaps). German athletes commonly use batch-tested products (e.g., Kölner Liste), which is background knowledge only.
