## 1. Which supplements dominated fitness and biohacking social media in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Creatine is the most-discussed mainstream supplement of 2025–2026. It has moved beyond gym culture to women, "brain health" and healthy aging, and it now sells as gummies too. Colostrum, magnesium, electrolytes, NMN and other "longevity" molecules, shilajit, lion's mane, collagen and ashwagandha/berberine are also strong. At the fringe of biohacking, 2025–2026 was dominated by methylene blue and injectable "research" peptides (BPC-157, TB-500). In the US, peptides got a further boost from HHS/FDA policy moves in 2026 that do not apply in the EU.

### Inferences
- The 2025–2026 feed has three tiers:
  1. Sports basics rebranded "for everyone": creatine for women, brain and aging; electrolytes; magnesium; protein.
  2. Podcast-driven "longevity" and "hormone" products: NMN/NAD+, urolithin A, spermidine, taurine, tongkat ali, shilajit, turkesterone, colostrum.
  3. Fringe biohacking with drug-like substances: methylene blue, BPC-157/TB-500 and other injectable peptides. This tier carries the highest legal, health and doping risk.
- US deregulation (NMN in September 2025, peptides in February–July 2026) produces a lot of US content that German users see. EU and German law did not change in parallel (see Q3). For a German trainee, "it's legal now" in a US video usually does **not** apply in Germany.

## 2. Which well-known influencer stacks circulate (podcast and biohacker protocols, "Blueprint"-style regimens) and what do they contain?

### Takeaway
The widely copied stacks are:
- Bryan Johnson's **Blueprint**: 100+ compounds a day. He dropped rapamycin after side effects. Secondary sources say he runs NMN 6 days a week and added low-dose lithium and NDGA in 2026.
- **Huberman's sleep stack**: magnesium L-threonate, L-theanine and apigenin.
- **Huberman's "testosterone stack"**: tongkat ali 400 mg, *Fadogia agrestis* 600 mg, zinc and boron.
- **Sinclair-style NMN + resveratrol.**
- **Methylene blue** as pushed by biohackers such as Ben Greenfield.
- **Peptide "healing stacks"** (BPC-157 + TB-500).

Each stack contains one or two components with some human evidence, padded with animal- or mechanism-only ingredients. Some contain prescription drugs or substances that are unauthorised in the EU or prohibited in sport.

### Inferences
- The typical influencer stack combines:
  - a few defensible basics: creatine; magnesium if intake is low; L-theanine;
  - ingredients with only animal or mechanistic data: apigenin, fadogia, NDGA, NMN "for longevity";
  - at the extreme end, drugs: rapamycin, lithium, methylene blue, peptides.

  The stack as a whole is almost never trial-tested.
- Johnson's own rapamycin exit, with infections, worse lipids and glucose and a higher resting heart rate, is a strong counter-example to "n=1 optimisation": even the most-monitored biohacker saw harm.
- A German trainee who copies these stacks will end up buying:
  - EU-unauthorised novel foods: tongkat ali; NMN until the Commission acts;
  - substances that are neither food nor authorised consumer medicine: methylene blue capsules, BPC-157;
  - untested combinations at US-level doses: NMN at 1–2 g versus EFSA's 300 mg/day safety level.

## 3. For each product: claim, human evidence as of 2026, typical dose, side effects and interactions, EU/DE legal status (novel food, medicine) and WADA/NADA status

### Takeaway
Only **creatine monohydrate powder** clearly delivers on its core performance claim. The EU has authorised health claims at 3 g/day, one of them for people over 55 who do resistance training. The "creatine for your brain or depression" claims rest on small or very-low-certainty effects, and many **creatine gummies contain almost no creatine**.

Several products are **situational** only: electrolytes for long or hot sessions and heavy sweaters; colostrum at research doses (20–60 g/day) during hard training.

Most longevity and hormone trend products have null, tiny or no human evidence:
- turkesterone: null RCT in 2025;
- spermidine: null 12-month RCT;
- taurine: the aging premise was undercut in 2025;
- apigenin: no human sleep trial;
- AG1: no independent RCT;
- collagen for skin: benefits appear only in industry-funded trials.

Several are **not legal as food in the EU**:
- tongkat ali: unauthorised novel food; EFSA raised genotoxicity concerns in 2021;
- NMN: EFSA gave a positive opinion in May 2026, but Commission authorisation was still pending in the sources;
- isolated fulvic acid in shilajit;
- methylene blue;
- BPC-157.

BPC-157 is also **prohibited at all times (WADA S0)**, and TB-500 is prohibited under S2. Ecdysterone is on WADA's monitoring program.

### Cited Findings

#### General framework (doping and contamination)
- The WADA 2026 Prohibited List and Monitoring Program have been in force since 2026-01-01. [IWBF](https://www.iwbf.org/news/wadas-2026-prohibited-list-now-in-force) (sports federation; 2026)
- S0 ("non-approved substances") covers any drug not approved by a government health authority for human use, including designer drugs, discontinued pharmaceuticals and veterinary-only compounds. [Athletics Integrity Unit](https://www.athleticsintegrity.org/know-the-rules/understand-the-prohibited-list) (anti-doping body; n.d.)
- 2026 additions include two substances "found in supplements and products marketed as 'research chemicals'": 2-phenylbenzo[h]chromen-4-one (an aromatase inhibitor) and BAM15 (described as acting on AMP-activated protein kinase). Clarifications were added to S1, S2 and S4, and non-diagnostic carbon monoxide was added (M1.4). [USADA athlete advisory – 2026 list](https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/2026-wada-prohibited-list/) (anti-doping agency; 2025/26); [UEFA](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/02a0-1f7401c8fb58-7db33c514e3e-1000--wada-list-of-prohibited-substances-for-2026/) (sports federation; 2026)
- **Kölner Liste** (the German list of supplements tested for doping substances, integrated into the NADA app):
  - Testing only *minimises* the risk of contamination or falsification; individual batches can still be contaminated.
  - Supplements can contain prohibited substances such as higenamine, methylhexanamine, oxilofrine or sibutramine, added deliberately or picked up during filling.
  - The list is not a recommendation, and NADA generally advises athletes against regular supplement use without an indication and consultation with a physician or Olympic-centre nutritionist.

  [Kölner Liste – Hintergrund](https://www.koelnerliste.com/hintergrund/) (NADA-affiliated initiative; n.d.); [NADA – "Vorsicht vor Nahrungsergänzungsmitteln"](https://www.nada.de/en/service/news/news-detail/vorsicht-vor-nahrungsergaenzungsmitteln) (national anti-doping agency; n.d.); [Verbraucherzentrale – "Gewonnen und trotzdem verloren – ungewollt gedopt"](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/lebensmittel/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/gewonnen-und-trotzdem-verloren-ungewollt-gedopt-13400) (German consumer agency; n.d.)

#### Creatine ("for everything") and creatine gummies
- **EU health claims:** two claims are authorised.
  1. Creatine increases physical performance in successive bursts of short-term, high-intensity exercise, for adults doing high-intensity exercise who take **3 g/day**.
  2. Daily creatine enhances the effect of resistance training on muscle strength in **adults over 55**.

  [Verbraucherzentrale – Kreatin](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/lebensmittel/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/kreatin-nur-in-seltenen-faellen-hilfreich-8089) (German consumer agency; n.d.; the URL slug reads "Kreatin nur in seltenen Fällen hilfreich"); [PTA-Forum – "Was bringt Kreatin"](https://www.pta-forum.de/pharmazie-medizin/was-bringt-kreatin/) (German pharmacy trade press; n.d.)
- Creatine helps with repeated short bursts of intense activity (sprinting, weightlifting) and "appears to have little value" for endurance. [Harvard T.H. Chan – Nutrition Source: Workout supplements](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/workout-supplements) (academic; n.d.)
- **Cognition, 2024 meta-analysis** (Frontiers in Nutrition): no significant improvement in overall cognitive function or executive function. It did find significant positive effects on memory, attention time and processing speed, with larger benefits in people with disease, those aged 18–60 and females. A corrigendum was later published. [PubMed 39070254](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39070254/); [Frontiers full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1424972/full); [Corrigendum, PMC11873458](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11873458/) (peer-reviewed meta-analysis; 2024)
- **Cognition, RCT** (preprint, 2023, older): Bayesian evidence supported a *small* benefit. The effect bordered on significance for backward digit span, was absent for Raven's matrices, and there was no improvement on exploratory tasks. [medRxiv 2023](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.04.05.23288194.full.pdf) (preprint)
- **2026 German pharmacy press headline:** "Kreatin kann doch keine Kognition" (roughly "Creatine can't do cognition after all"). [Deutsche Apotheker Zeitung, 2026-04-28](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2026/04/28/kreatin-kann-doch-keine-kognition) (trade press; **headline only; content not accessible**). A 2026 systematic review, "Creatine and Cognition in Aging", appeared in *Nutrition Reviews* 84(2). [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/84/2/333/8253584) (peer-reviewed; 2026; conclusions not captured)
- **Depression, 2025 meta-analysis** (Eckert, Lima, Dariva; *British Journal of Nutrition*; searches up to February 2025): 11 trials with 1,093 participants. The pooled SMD was −0.34, equal to 2.2 points on the HAM-D-17, which is *below* the minimal important difference of 3.0. GRADE certainty was **very low**, and the review was reported as "inconclusive". [PubMed 41189312](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41189312/) (peer-reviewed; 2025); [News-Medical, 2025-11-06](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20251106/Can-creatine-help-with-depression-New-review-says-results-are-inconclusive.aspx) (science news)
  - The search summary contradicts itself: it says both "statistically significant but small reduction" and "confidence interval included zero". Treat the result as uncertain.
- **Kidneys:** creatinine can rise without kidney damage, and people with kidney disease should consult a doctor first. [megamax.de](https://megamax.de/blogs/infos/kreatin-und-kreatininwerte) / [Nordic Oil](https://www.nordicoil.de/blogs/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/kreatin-nebenwirkungen-mythen-nieren-blaehungen-dehydrierung) (**vendor, weak**; n.d.)
- **Gummies, SuppCo lab test (2025):** 5 popular powders passed, but **4 of 6 gummies failed** identity and potency testing. Reaching a 5 g dose would take about **800 gummies** of DivinusLabs or about **2,000 of Happyummmm**. These are "fairy-dusted" products delivering 0.50% and 0.09% of a standard dose. [SuppCo Tested](https://supp.co/articles/suppco-tested-creatine-testing-results-gummies-failed-lab-analysis) (supplement-rating company; 2025; snippet only, page blocked)
- **Gummies, NOW Foods tests:** a **~46% failure rate** across the creatine gummy brands tested. [Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/now-tests-creatine-gummies-identifies-limitations-in-third-party-gummy-testing); [Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/breaking-news/now-reports-widespread-failings-in-creatine-gummy-tests/) (trade press; **date not visible**. NOW is a competing manufacturer, which is a possible conflict of interest.)
- **Why gummies fail:** creatine is stable as a powder, but in water it converts to creatinine, and gummies are made with water, so they are hard to dose correctly. #### Tongkat ali (*Eurycoma longifolia*), plus *Fadogia agrestis*
- **Meta-analysis (2022, older):** 9 studies, including 5 RCTs, showed a significant increase in total testosterone in healthy volunteers and hypogonadal men. The authors say more research is needed before clinical use. [PubMed 36013514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36013514/) (peer-reviewed)
- **Examine** (status 2025-09-04): 27 references, 937 participants, 8 trials and 1 meta-analysis. It judges that "the data aren't convincing" (the search summary attributes this quote to Examine but does not tie it to the page explicitly). A 2025 study at **400 mg/day for 4 weeks** found no between-group differences in salivary free testosterone or cortisol versus placebo. [Examine – Tongkat ali research](https://examine.com/supplements/tongkat-ali/research/) (evidence database; 2025-09-04; **snippet only**; the 2025-study sentence may come from another result in the same search)
- Most human trials are small (under 100 subjects) and short (4–12 weeks), and effects are largest in men with low baseline testosterone. [MitoHealth guide 2026](https://mitohealth.com/guide/tongkat-ali-testosterone-guide) (**vendor, weak**)
- **EU legal status:** tongkat ali is an **unauthorised novel food** ("popular but illegal"). [NutraIngredients, 2016-09-06](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2016/09/06/Popular-but-illegal-Legal-certainty-on-the-horizon-for-Tongkat-ali-extract/) (trade press; older)
  - EFSA's NDA Panel (2021) assessed a standardised water root extract proposed for food supplements at up to 200 mg/day. In-vitro chromosome aberration tests were **positive (clastogenic)**, which raised safety concerns. #### Taurine
- **Science (2025, NIH-led):** circulating taurine does **not** decline with age in healthy humans, monkeys or mice; levels increase or stay the same. Differences between people are larger than the change over a lifetime, and associations with health are inconsistent, so taurine is **not a reliable aging biomarker**. The study used the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging, a Balearic Islands cohort and Emory's PREMED cohort. [Science – "Is taurine an aging biomarker?"](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adl2116) (peer-reviewed; 2025); [NIH news release](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-researchers-conclude-taurine-unlikely-be-good-aging-biomarker) (government; 2025); [NIH Record, 2025-07-18](https://nihrecord.nih.gov/2025/07/18/taurine-not-reliable-aging-biomarker)
- A 2025 paper in *Aging Cell* is titled "Experimental Evidence Against Taurine Deficiency as a Driver of Aging in Humans". [Marcangeli et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/acel.70191) (peer-reviewed; title only)

- Whether mushroom coffee delivers a cognitive dose at all is questioned. [Takeroon – "Mushroom Coffee: Does It Actually Deliver a Cognitive Dose?"](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/mushroom-coffee-science-cognitive-dose) (**vendor**; title only)

#### Beef organ capsules (desiccated liver and organs)
- **Vitamin A:** 100 g of cooked beef liver contains about 9,440 µg RAE of vitamin A, more than ten times the recommended intake. This is preformed retinol, which accumulates, and toxicity can develop slowly. [Superpower – beef liver supplements](https://superpower.com/guides/specialty-supplements/beef-liver-supplements-benefits) / [Nutriest](https://nutriest.eu/side-effects-of-beef-liver-supplements/) (**vendor, weak**; n.d.)
- **Pregnancy:** liver products are risky because excess preformed vitamin A causes birth defects, so pregnant people should avoid them. [HealthyMag, 2026-07-14](https://healthymag.org/2026/07/14/beef-organ-supplements/) (health blog; **weak**)
- **Evidence and labelling:** direct peer-reviewed evidence for desiccated liver capsules in healthy adults is "essentially absent", and most labels omit exact vitamin A and copper amounts. [HealthyMag 2026](https://healthymag.org/2026/07/14/beef-organ-supplements/); [WellnessPulse – Heart & Soil review](https://wellnesspulse.com/nutrition/heart-and-soil-supplements-review/) (review sites; **weak**)

#### Collagen
- **Myung & Park 2025** (*American Journal of Medicine*): 23 RCTs with 1,474 participants. **Industry-funded studies showed benefits** for skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles; **independent studies showed no effect on any parameter**. 21 of the 23 trials had some industry involvement. [NutraIngredients, 2025-08-26](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/08/26/industry-reacts-to-meta-analysis-concluding-collagen-supplements-show-no-proven-benefit-for-skin-aging/) (trade press); [iGlowly editorial](https://www.iglowly.com/en/insights/collagen-supplements-show-benefits-only-in-industry-funded-studies) (n.d.)
- **Industry rebuttal:** "industry-funded studies are not necessarily of lower quality"; most scored 3–5 out of 5 on the Jadad scale. Critics also note that there are very few independent trials and some used suboptimal doses or durations. [Collagen Stewardship Alliance statement](https://collagenalliance.org/csa-statement-on-2025-collagen-meta-analysis/) (industry body; 2025); [Tosla whitepaper](https://whitepapers.toslanutricosmetics.com/why-the-latest-meta-analysis-on-collagen-misses-the-mark/) (industry)

Further inferences:
- **Legal traps for Germans:** several "must-haves" from US podcasts are not legally marketable as food in the EU: NMN (until the Commission acts), tongkat ali, isolated fulvic acid in shilajit, methylene blue and BPC-157. Online shops still sell them. That they are available does not mean they are legal or quality-controlled.
- **Doping traps:** only BPC-157 and TB-500 are explicitly prohibited among the listed products. Ecdysterone is monitored. The broader risk for tested athletes is **contamination** (higenamine, methylhexanamine, sibutramine) and the "research chemical" category, which WADA extended in 2026 with BAM15 and an aromatase inhibitor found in supplements.
- **Dose and format mismatch is a recurring pattern:** creatine gummies are fairy-dusted; colostrum is sold at 3 g versus 20–60 g in studies; NMN is taken at 1–2 g versus EFSA's 300 mg; AG1's proprietary blends hide doses.
- **Evidence reversals in 2025–2026** are the strongest counter to hype: taurine (*Science* 2025), turkesterone (null RCT 2025), collagen for skin (independent trials null, 2025), creatine for cognition (sceptical DAZ headline 2026; very-low-certainty depression evidence 2025) and Johnson's own rapamycin exit.

## 4. What do sceptical expert voices and community discussions conclude?

### Takeaway
Sceptical experts and evidence-focused outlets converge on "boring basics work": protein, creatine monohydrate, caffeine, and electrolytes only when sweat losses are high. Trend products are criticised as over-marketed, under-dosed, contaminated or unproven. German pharmacy and consumer bodies (ABDA, Verbraucherzentrale, NADA, DAZ, PZ) are explicitly critical of methylene blue, NMN, shilajit and indiscriminate supplement use. Reddit, X and YouTube discussions could not be accessed, so community sentiment is only indirectly documented.

### Inferences
- Across independent experts, the evidence hierarchy is consistent. **Creatine powder** is at the top for strength and power, with **protein** and **caffeine**. **Electrolytes** and **colostrum** are niche, for very high sweat losses or heavy training blocks. The rest is marketing-led.
- The sceptical German institutional voices (Verbraucherzentrale, NADA, ABDA) are *more* restrictive than US commentators. Verbraucherzentrale is cautious even about creatine outside high-intensity sport. For a German trainee this is the more relevant reference frame because of the legal differences.
- Influencer claims are often "technically true but irrelevant": an effect exists only in hypogonadal men (tongkat ali), only at 10–20 times the influencer dose (colostrum), only in industry-funded trials (collagen), only in mice (taurine, fadogia) or only in 12-person trials of older adults (GlyNAC).

- **Stiftung Warentest and Öko-Test:** no 2025–2026 German tests of the trend products were found (for example creatine gummies, NMN, shilajit).
