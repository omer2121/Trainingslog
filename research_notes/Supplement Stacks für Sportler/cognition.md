## 1. Caffeine + L-theanine: doses, ratio, effects on attention and jitteriness, tolerance

### Takeaway
Caffeine is the only ingredient in this file with strong evidence at the level of position stands. It acutely improves alertness, attention and vigilance, including during sleep loss, at about 1–3 mg/kg. EFSA sets the limits at 200 mg per single dose and 400 mg/day. In daily users, however, much of the felt effect is reversal of withdrawal, and caffeine costs sleep unless taken about 9–13 h before bed. Adding L-theanine (about 100–250 mg) gives small-to-moderate extra benefits on attention tasks in the 1st–2nd hour in pooled data (Payne 2025). The confidence intervals are wide, the "anti-jitter" claim is poorly supported, and a 2026 crossover trial in athletes found no synergy.

### Cited Findings

**Caffeine: EU safety limits**
- EFSA NDA Panel, May 2015:
  - Single doses up to 200 mg (about 3 mg/kg for a 70 kg adult) raise no safety concern in healthy adults. This also holds when taken less than 2 h before intense exercise under normal environmental conditions.
  - Habitual intake up to 400 mg/day raises no concern for non-pregnant adults. The limit in pregnancy is 200 mg/day.
  - A single 100 mg dose (about 1.4 mg/kg) may affect sleep duration and sleep patterns in some adults, especially close to bedtime.
  - Children and adolescents: 3 mg/kg/day.
  - Sources: [EFSA Journal 2015;13(5):4102](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4102); [EUFIC summary](https://www.eufic.org/en/whats-in-food/article/efsa-opinion-on-the-safety-of-caffeine); [Nutritional Outlook](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/efsa-says-caffeine-safe-eu-adults-400-mg-daily-or-200-mg-single-dose) [S; OLD, but still the current EU reference as far as I found]

**Caffeine: performance and cognition**
- ISSN position stand (Guest et al., January 2021):
  - Caffeine consistently improves exercise performance at 3–6 mg/kg. The minimum effective dose is unclear and may be as low as 2 mg/kg. The most common timing is 60 min before exercise.
  - Aerobic endurance shows the most consistent moderate-to-large benefit. Responses vary widely between individuals.
  - In most studies caffeine improves cognitive function, including attention and vigilance.
  - Under sleep deprivation it "may improve cognitive and physical performance in some individuals", but not indefinitely.
  - Sources: [JISSN 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4); [PMC7777221](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7777221/) [S]
- The ISSN also published a separate position stand on coffee and sports performance in 2023 — [JISSN 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15502783.2023.2237952) [S; content not retrieved]

**Caffeine: sleep cost (relevant for evening training)**
- Gardiner et al. 2023 (Sleep Medicine Reviews; systematic review and meta-analysis of 24 studies):
  - Caffeine cut total sleep time by 45 min and sleep efficiency by 7%. It lengthened time to fall asleep by 9 min and wake time after sleep onset by 12 min.
  - Light sleep (N1) increased; deep sleep (N3/N4) decreased.
  - To avoid losing sleep time, drink a coffee (107 mg per 250 mL) at least 8.8 h before bed, and take a standard pre-workout serving (217.5 mg) at least 13.2 h before bed.
  - The sleep loss shrank by 2.8 min for each extra hour between intake and bedtime.
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079223000205); [Leeds Beckett repository PDF](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/9625/1/TheEffectOfCaffeineOnSubsequentSleepASystematicReviewAndMetaanalysisPV-WEAKLEY.pdf); [AJMC summary](https://www.ajmc.com/view/caffeine-and-sleep-review-suggests-consumption-guidelines) [S]
- A 2025 meta-analysis of controlled crossover trials looked at caffeine's effects on sleep by age and dose — [Sleep Medicine 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1389945725005490) [S; results not retrieved]

**Caffeine: tolerance and "withdrawal reversal" [CONTESTED]**
- Rogers et al. 2013 ("Faster but not smarter", Psychopharmacology):
  - Frequent consumers who have briefly abstained become more alert and perform better after caffeine. Neither their alertness nor their performance exceeds that of non-consumers given placebo.
  - Caffeine had no significant net enhancing effect on performance, either acutely or after habitual use. This supports the withdrawal-reversal hypothesis.
  - Caffeine most likely helps mood and performance when alertness is low.
  - Source: [Psychopharmacology 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-012-2889-4) [S; this finding and Rogers 2014 came from the same combined search summary, so which sentence belongs to which paper is uncertain]
- Rogers 2014 (Journal of Caffeine Research): defends withdrawal reversal as the explanation for the alerting effect in frequent consumers — [Liebert](https://www.liebertpub.com/doi/10.1089/jcr.2014.0009) [S; OLD]
- James & Rogers 2005: caffeine-induced cognitive enhancement is confounded by reversal of withdrawal effects — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00049530500125090) [S; OLD]
- A 2005 trial tested caffeine and caffeine withdrawal on mood and cognition impaired by sleep restriction — [PubMed 15887055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15887055/) [S; results not retrieved; OLD]

**Caffeine + L-theanine: pooled evidence**
- Payne, Aceves-Martins, Dubost, Greyling & de Roos (Nutrition Reviews 83(10):1873; published online May 2025; systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs in healthy participants):
  - 50 RCTs were included; 15 were eligible for at least one meta-analysis.
  - Compared with placebo, theanine + caffeine showed small-to-moderate differences in the 1st and 2nd hour after intake. These favoured the combination for choice reaction time, digit-vigilance accuracy, attention-switching accuracy and overall mood.
  - Conclusion: the combination "likely confers small-to-moderate improvements in attentional task performance ... primarily in the second hour".
  - However, the confidence intervals were "frequently consistent with little-to-no difference".
  - Sources: [Nutrition Reviews](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/83/10/1873/8123998); [Proc Nutr Soc abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-nutrition-society/article/effects-of-tea-camellia-sinensis-or-its-bioactive-compounds-ltheanine-or-ltheanine-plus-caffeine-on-cognition-sleep-and-mood-in-healthy-participants-a-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-of-randomised-controlled-trials/1F0ABB804FC3E74DCC0799E0403627DD) [S; funding and conflicts of interest not checked]
- According to a vendor blog, subjective alertness was not significant in this 2025 pooled analysis (the confidence interval crossed zero) — [takeroon blog](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/caffeine-and-l-theanine-ratio) [S; WEAK: nicotine-pouch vendor]
- Camfield et al. 2014 (Nutrition Reviews; meta-analysis of acute effects of tea constituents): moderate effect sizes favoured combined caffeine + L-theanine in the first 2 h for alertness and attention-switching accuracy — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/nure.12120); [PubMed 24946991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24946991/) [S; OLD]
- A systematic review (PMC8794723, about 2021/22):
  - L-theanine alone improved self-reported relaxation, tension and calmness from 200 mg upwards.
  - Most of the attention-enhancing effect was attributed to caffeine.
  - Source: [PMC8794723](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8794723/) [S; attribution comes from a combined search summary]
- ADDF Cognitive Vitality report: L-theanine may improve the ability to ignore distractors. Together with caffeine it reduced mind wandering during a demanding task — [ADDF L-theanine PDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/L-Theanine-Cognitive-Vitality-For-Researchers.pdf) [S]
- Newer reviews exist but were not retrieved:
  - 2025 meta-analysis on L-theanine alone ("Promising, but not completely conclusive") — [PMC12609247](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12609247/)
  - 2026 Frontiers review of L-theanine and cognition, mood and sleep — [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1922819/full) [S; titles only]

**Individual trials, doses and ratio**
- Owen et al. 2008: 50 mg caffeine + 100 mg L-theanine improved speed and accuracy on an attention-switching task within 60–90 min and raised subjective alertness — [paper title on ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47643925_The_combination_of_L-theanine_and_caffeine_improves_cognitive_performance_and_increases_subjective_alertness); details from [takeroon blog](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/theanine-to-caffeine-ratio-trials) [S; details WEAK; OLD]
- Haskell et al. 2008: 250 mg L-theanine + 150 mg caffeine improved sentence verification, simple reaction time, numeric working-memory reaction time and delayed word-recognition reaction time versus placebo, and increased alertness. Neither compound alone did so — [takeroon blog](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/theanine-to-caffeine-ratio-trials) [S; WEAK secondary source; OLD]
- Giesbrecht et al. 2010 (Appetite), titled "L-theanine and caffeine improve task switching but not intersensory attention or subjective alertness"; low doses, about 97 mg theanine + 40 mg caffeine [PK] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0195666310000267) [S; OLD]
- Ratio: the popular 2:1 theanine-to-caffeine ratio is a marketing convention. The roughly 8 combination trials used ratios from 0.67:1 to 2.4:1, and absolute doses varied about threefold — [takeroon blog](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/theanine-to-caffeine-ratio-trials) [S; WEAK]
- Athlete trial (Frontiers in Nutrition, published 26 January 2026):
  - Design: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover in 20 trained competitive athletes. Four conditions: caffeine 3 mg/kg, theanine 200 mg, both together, and placebo, with at least 72 h washout.
  - Outcomes: maximal strength, intermittent aerobic endurance and eye–hand coordination.
  - Effects were task-specific and small. Caffeine alone did not differ from placebo on any outcome. The authors say this "challenges the prevailing assumption of CAF + TEA synergy".
  - Sources: [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1751673/full); [PubMed 41668720](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41668720/); [NCT07268573](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07268573) [S]
- A 2025 study of caffeine + L-theanine in Iranian elite wrestlers (cognitive and sport-specific outcomes) exists — [JISSN 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15502783.2025.2564238) [S; results not retrieved]

**L-theanine: legal status in Germany and the EU**
- The BfR (German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment) rejected adding isolated L-theanine to drinks. Its reason: too little is known about the wanted and unwanted effects of isolated L-theanine in humans. Isolated L-theanine in drinks was not approved in Germany — [BfR PDF "Getränke mit isoliertem L-Theanin"](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/343/getraenke_mit_isoliertem_l_theanin.pdf); [BfR A–Z entry](https://www.bfr.bund.de/de/a-z_index/l_theanin-5019.html) [S; date of opinion not retrieved, possibly OLD]
- Novel-food status reportedly depends on how L-theanine is sourced (extracted from tea vs synthetic or fermented). L-theanine capsules are sold in Germany, and an EU novel-food application was reportedly under review — [nutraceuticalsgroup](https://nutraceuticalsgroup.com/de/news/l-theanine-synthetic-vs-natural-and-its-classification-as-a-novel-food) [S; WEAK]
- The BVL (German Federal Office of Consumer Protection and Food Safety) is the competent German authority for novel foods. Food businesses must determine a product's novel-food status themselves — [BVL Novel Food](https://www.bvl.bund.de/DE/Arbeitsbereiche/01_Lebensmittel/03_Verbraucher/06_NovelFood/lm_NovelFood_node.html) [S]

### Inferences
- A practical caffeine range for a 70 kg adult is 1–3 mg/kg (about 75–200 mg) per occasion, up to 400 mg/day. This stays within EFSA's single-dose limit and the ISSN's effective range.
- Using Gardiner 2023's cut-offs with a 22:30 bedtime, about 100 mg should be finished by about 13:40, and a 200 mg+ pre-workout by about 09:20. Caffeine before evening training is therefore a common hidden sleep cost.
- In habitual daily users, much of the felt effect is a return to baseline. The largest net benefit is likely when alertness is low: sleep loss, early shifts, monotonous vigilance tasks. This argues for keeping daily intake moderate and saving higher doses for when they matter. No trial of "caffeine cycling for cognition" was found.
- L-theanine is a low-risk add-on (100–200 mg with 50–150 mg caffeine, ratio about 1:1 to 2:1) if caffeine makes the user tense. The expected cognitive gain is small and uncertain, and the 2026 athlete trial found no synergy on physical or sport-type tasks.
- "Anti-jitter" is plausible but weakly supported. The solid data are on attention-switching accuracy and overall mood. L-theanine alone reduces self-rated tension at 200 mg or more, but no trial found here shows it cancels caffeine-induced tremor or anxiety.

### Gaps
- No full-text access, so the pooled standardized mean differences (SMDs) for Payne 2025 and Camfield 2014 are missing.
- No robust trial evidence was found that L-theanine reduces caffeine-induced jitteriness, anxiety or heart rate. These claims come from marketing sources.
- Whether tolerance to caffeine's vigilance benefit is complete or partial is still contested. Only a low-quality summary site claimed that the vigilance benefit partly persists.
- Not verified this session: EU caffeine labelling rules ("high caffeine content" wording for drinks above 150 mg/L) and the German OTC or pharmacy status of caffeine tablets.
- Not verified: the exact EU Novel Food Catalogue entry for L-theanine, and whether any authorisation was granted by 2026.

## 2. Creatine for cognition: sleep deprivation, stress, older adults, vegetarians; 2024–2026 evidence; dose

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses from 2023 and 2024 show small memory benefits (SMD about 0.3). Results are heterogeneous, and the two analyses disagree about which age groups benefit. EFSA formally rejected a cognition health claim in November 2024. The most relevant new evidence is two small German trials (2024 and 2026) from the same research group: a single high dose (0.2–0.35 g/kg) reduced cognitive decline during a night without sleep, with the peak effect about 4 h after intake. The usual 3–5 g/day may be too low for reliable brain effects in people who eat meat. Vegetarians, older adults, and people who are stressed or sleep-deprived are the most plausible responders.

### Cited Findings

**Meta-analyses**
- Xu et al. 2024 (Frontiers in Nutrition 11:1424972; 16 RCTs; 492 participants aged 20.8–76.4 years, healthy and diseased; all used creatine monohydrate):
  - Significant effects on memory (SMD 0.31, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.44), attention time (SMD −0.31, −0.58 to −0.03) and processing-speed time (SMD −0.51, −1.01 to −0.01).
  - No significant effect on overall cognitive function (SMD 0.22, −0.40 to 0.84; I² 61%; p=0.49) or on executive function.
  - Subgroups: more benefit in people with diseases, those aged 18–60, and females.
  - Sources: [Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1424972/full); [PubMed 39070254](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39070254/) [S]
- A corrigendum (2025) and a published commentary (2026) on this meta-analysis exist; their content was not retrieved — [Corrigendum 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1570800/full); [Commentary 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1716285/full) [S; titles only]
- Prokopidis et al. 2023 (Nutrition Reviews 81(4):416; meta-analysis of RCTs in healthy people):
  - Creatine improved memory: SMD 0.29 (95% CI 0.04 to 0.53).
  - Older adults aged 66–76: SMD 0.88 (0.22 to 1.55). Younger people aged 11–31: SMD 0.03 (−0.14 to 0.20), not significant.
  - Source: [Nutrition Reviews](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/81/4/416/6671817) [title S; numbers PK, verify]
- [CONTESTED] The age findings conflict. Prokopidis 2023 finds the benefit mainly in older adults; Xu 2024 finds more benefit at age 18–60 — see the sources above.

**EFSA health claim (EU)**
- EFSA NDA Panel opinion (EFSA Journal 22(11):e9100, 19 November 2024; applicant Alzchem Trostberg GmbH via Austria; Article 13(5)):
  - Conclusion: a cause-and-effect relationship between creatine and improved cognitive function has **not** been established.
  - Two studies showed an acute working-memory effect at 20 g/day for 5–7 days. This was not seen at 2.2–14 g/day, nor with 5 g/day for 6 weeks after a 5-day loading phase.
  - An effect on response inhibition at 20 g/day for 7 days was an isolated finding among 10 studies in healthy people.
  - Three studies in people with disease did not support an effect, and the evidence for a mechanism was judged weak.
  - Sources: [EFSA Journal 2024](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2024.9100); [PubMed 39564533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39564533/); [NutraIngredients Dec 2024](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/12/12/stakeholders-react-to-efsas-health-claim-rejection-of-creatine-for-cognitive-function/); [NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/efsa-health-panel-dismisses-claims-linking-creatine-and-improved-cognitive-function.html) [S]

**Sleep deprivation: a single high dose**
- Gordji-Nejad et al. 2024 (Scientific Reports, February 2024; Forschungszentrum Jülich):
  - 15 participants took a single dose of 0.35 g/kg creatine monohydrate during overnight sleep deprivation.
  - Brain energy measures (31P-MRS) changed: PCr/Pi, ATP and tCr/tNAA shifted, and the drop in brain pH was prevented.
  - Performance improved on word memory and processing speed, and on language, logic and numeric tasks.
  - The effect peaked about 4 h after intake and lasted up to 9 h.
  - Sources: [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9); [FZ Jülich press release](https://www.fz-juelich.de/en/news/archive/press-release/2024/creatine-improves-cognitive-performance-during-sleep-deprivation); [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2024-04-creatine-cognitive-deprivation.html) [S]
- Gordji-Nejad et al. 2026 (Nutrients 18(8):1192, April 2026; German institutions):
  - 29 healthy participants took a single dose of 0.2 g/kg creatine monohydrate or placebo during 21 h of sleep deprivation. They were tested at an evening baseline and 3, 5.5 and 7.5 h after the dose.
  - Creatine reduced the decline in logical and numerical tasks, in language-related processing speed, and on the Psychomotor Vigilance Test.
  - Cognitive performance was about 12% better.
  - Sources: [MDPI Nutrients 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/8/1192); [PubMed 42075005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42075005/); [NutraIngredients Apr 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/04/21/single-dose-creatine-may-support-cognition-in-sleep-deprivation-study/) [S; blinding and design details not retrieved]

**Older adults and clinical populations**
- Older adults (68–85 years) given 20 g/day for 7 days improved on memory measures versus placebo: forward number recall, forward and backward spatial recall, and long-term memory. This was cited in a search summary alongside [Prokopidis 2023](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/81/4/416/6671817) [S; attribution uncertain; the primary study is OLD]
- CABA pilot trial (Smith et al., Alzheimer's & Dementia: TRCI, 2025):
  - Single-arm, no placebo group: 20 g/day creatine monohydrate for 8 weeks in 20 patients with Alzheimer's disease. 19 reached at least 80% compliance.
  - Blood creatine rose, and total brain creatine increased by 11%.
  - Cognition improved on the global composite (p=.02) and fluid composite (p=.004), List Sorting (p=.001), Oral Reading (p<.001) and Flanker (p=.05).
  - Sources: [Alzheimer's & Dementia TRCI 2025](https://alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/trc2.70101); [PubMed 40395689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40395689/); [KUMC news](https://www.kumc.edu/about/news/news-archive/creatine-alzheimers-research.html) [S]

**Vegetarians**
- Rae et al. 2003: 5 g/day for 6 weeks improved working memory in vegetarians. Benton & Donohoe 2011: 20 g/day for 5 days improved memory more in vegetarians than in omnivores. Vegetarians have lower creatine in blood and muscle, but similar total brain creatine — [PubMed 21118604 (Benton & Donohoe 2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21118604/); [Vegan Health](https://veganhealth.org/protein/creatine/) [S; OLD]
- Sandkühler et al. 2023 (BMC Medicine) ran a randomized controlled trial of creatine and cognition in vegetarians and omnivores — [BMC Medicine 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12916-023-03146-5) [S; results not retrieved; see Gaps]

### Inferences
- Evidence grade:
  - Healthy young omnivores: memory benefit small and inconsistent.
  - Older adults and vegetarians: plausible, moderate.
  - Acute rescue during sleep deprivation: promising but early (two small trials, n=15 and n=29, by the same group).
- Dose: brain effects look dose-dependent. The trials that found effects used 20 g/day for 5–7 days, or a single dose of 0.2–0.35 g/kg (about 14–25 g for 70 kg). EFSA found no effect at 14 g/day or less, or at 5 g/day maintenance. The usual 3–5 g/day, which works for muscle, is not proven for cognition in people who eat meat.
- The acute high-dose protocol for sleep loss is experimental. Because the effect peaks 3–4 h after intake, it would have to be taken hours before the critical period. Gastrointestinal tolerance of 14–25 g in one dose is a practical concern that I did not verify.
- EFSA rejected the claim in 2024, so any product marketed in Germany as "creatine for brain/cognition" is making a non-authorised health claim.
- Creatine is still the best "dual-use" option for a trainee. The training benefit is strong, the cognitive upside is possible but small, and the risk at standard doses is low.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: safety and tolerability of single 14–25 g doses (gastrointestinal upset) and of 20 g/day long term.
- Stress models (hypoxia, mental fatigue, exam stress): no 2024–2026 trials were found in this session, and older studies were not retrieved.
- Sandkühler 2023 (BMC Medicine) results are unverified. From my existing knowledge [PK]: about 120 vegetarians and omnivores took 5 g/day for 6 weeks in a crossover design; there was at most a small effect on backward digit span and no extra benefit for vegetarians. Verify.
- The content of the 2025 corrigendum and the 2026 commentary on Xu 2024 is unknown. Either could change the numbers above.
- Not verified this session: the EU-authorised creatine claims for physical performance (believed to require 3 g/day).

## 3. Omega-3, bacopa, citicoline, alpha-GPC, ginkgo, ginseng, rhodiola, lion's mane, cocoa flavanols, B vitamins: meaningful effects in healthy young adults?

### Takeaway
None of these has robust, replicated effects in healthy young adults.
- **At best modest:**
  - Omega-3, and only if habitual DHA intake is low.
  - Bacopa: small memory gains after 12 weeks or more, with digestive side effects.
  - Citicoline: a few industry-funded trials, mostly in middle-aged and older adults. EU-authorised up to 500 mg/day.
- **Not recommended:**
  - Ginkgo: essentially zero effect in healthy people, plus bleeding-interaction risk.
  - Alpha-GPC: an observational stroke signal and unresolved EU novel-food status.
- **Too little evidence:** lion's mane, rhodiola and ginseng rest on small, inconsistent trials.
- **Only for specific groups:** cocoa flavanols and B vitamins help mainly people with poor diets or a deficiency.

### Cited Findings

#### Omega-3 (DHA/EPA)
- Scientific Reports 2025 (systematic review with dose-response meta-analysis, 58 studies): each additional 2000 mg/day of omega-3 was linked to better attention and perceptual speed; evidence quality varied — [Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-16129-8) [S; population mix and effect sizes not retrieved]
- Stonehouse et al. 2013 (AJCN; randomized controlled trial): 176 healthy adults aged 18–45 with low DHA intake took 1.16 g DHA/day for 6 months. Memory and reaction time improved — [PubMed 23515006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23515006/) [S; OLD]
- A 12-week trial of DHA-rich or EPA-rich fish oil in healthy 18–35-year-olds found no effect on cognition or mood. People with low habitual DHA intake, older adults with age-related decline and people with mild cognitive impairment seem to benefit most — [Stonehouse 2014 review (author PK), PMC4113767](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4113767/) [S; OLD]
- A search summary also stated that EPA-rich oil, but not DHA alone, improved global cognition in healthy young adults. The source of this claim is unclear [S; unverified]
- Safety: atrial fibrillation (AF)
  - Gencer et al. 2021 (Circulation; meta-analysis of 7 cardiovascular-outcome trials): more than 1 g/day of marine omega-3 was linked to a 49% higher AF risk — [Circulation 2021](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.055654); [HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/meta-analysis-raises-questions-around-risk-of-atrial-fibrillation-associated-with-omega-3-supplements) [S]
  - Another 2021 meta-analysis (Lombardi et al. [PK author]): RR 1.12 (1.04–1.21) at 1 g/day or less, and RR 1.51 (1.26–1.80) above 1 g/day — [PubMed 34057665](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34057665/) [S; linking these RR values to this paper relies on the search summary]
  - An updated meta-analysis (medRxiv preprint, December 2025; 34 trials; 114,326 people) found a significant AF increase only in high cardiovascular-risk patients taking more than 1500 mg/day EPA/DHA: OR 1.48 (1.21–1.81), absolute risk difference 0.8% — [medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.14.25342167v1.full) [S; preprint, not peer-reviewed]

#### Bacopa monnieri
- ADDF Cognitive Vitality review: in 6–9 trials of 3 months or longer, most cognitive scores did not change, with occasional benefits on memory and attention tests. Multi-ingredient products make effects hard to attribute. Side effects were mostly mild: digestive discomfort and flu-like symptoms such as nausea — [ADDF rating](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/cognitive-vitality/ratings/bacopa-monnieri); [ADDF PDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Bacopa_monnieri_UPDATE_%28supplements%29.pdf) [S]
- The typical dose is 300 mg/day of extract standardised to about 55% bacosides. A phase I study found 300 and 450 mg/day safe in healthy adults — [Examine](https://examine.com/supplements/bacopa-monnieri/) [S]
- In a randomized controlled trial, Bacopa caused no serious adverse reactions but more self-reported adverse reactions than placebo, mainly digestive — [search summary, probably the 2025 Bacumen trial, PubMed 41091332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41091332/) [S; attribution uncertain]
- A 2025 randomized controlled trial of Bacumen® (a Bacopa extract) on cognition, stress and fatigue in healthy adults exists — [PubMed 41091332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41091332/) [S; results not retrieved]
- EU status (UNVERIFIED): a search summary said Bacopa was already sold in EU food supplements before 1997, i.e. it is not novel in food supplements. A trade article reports that the EU updated the catalogue status of several plants — [supplement-experten.de](https://www.supplement-experten.de/supplemente-fuer-den-fokus/) [WEAK]; [Mérieux NutriSciences](https://www.merieuxnutrisciences.com/de/eu-aktualisiert-status-pflanzen-katalog-novel-food/) [S; not accessible]

#### Citicoline (CDP-choline)
- Industry trial (Cognizin/Kyowa): 148 healthy adults aged 35–75 with poor sustained attention took 500 mg/day or placebo for 12 weeks. Sustained attention and information-processing speed improved significantly (COMPASS test battery) — [Cognizin study page](https://cognizin.com/studies/effect-citicoline-supplementation-attention-healthy-adults-randomized-double-blind-placebo-controlled-clinical-trial); [Cognizin news](https://cognizin.com/news/post/citicoline-supplementation-attention-healthy-adults) [S; IND; publication year not retrieved]
- Nakazaki et al. 2021 (J Nutr; randomized controlled trial): 100 healthy adults aged 50–85 with age-associated memory impairment took 500 mg/day for 12 weeks. Overall and episodic memory improved — [PMC8349115](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8349115/); [Cognizin](https://cognizin.com/studies/cognizin-citicoline-improves-overall-memory-and-episodic-memory-in-healthy-older-adults) [S; IND]
- Citicoline–caffeine drink (Bruce et al. 2014; randomized controlled trial, 60 healthy adults): faster maze learning and reaction times, fewer go/no-go errors and better processing-speed accuracy versus placebo — [PubMed 25046515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25046515/) [S; OLD; citicoline's effect cannot be separated from caffeine's]
- A trial of acute and chronic 250 mg citicoline on attention is registered — [NCT05000190](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05000190) [S; results not retrieved]
- EU status: authorised novel food (Commission Implementing Decision 2014/423/EU, 1 July 2014; applicant Kyowa Hakko Europe GmbH, Düsseldorf):
  - Food supplements: up to 500 mg/day.
  - Foods for special medical purposes: up to 250 mg per serving and 1,000 mg/day.
  - Not permitted in foods for children.
  - EFSA had found it safe at the proposed uses in October 2013.
  - Sources: [EUR-Lex 32014D0423](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32014D0423); [NutraIngredients 2014](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2014/07/18/Citicoline-wins-EU-novel-foods-approval-in-medical-foods-and-food-supplements/) [S]

#### Alpha-GPC (L-α-glycerylphosphorylcholine)
- Lee et al. 2021 (JAMA Network Open 4(11):e2136008; Korean nationwide matched cohorts of more than 12 million people aged 50+ without prior stroke, Alzheimer's or cerebrovascular disease; 10-year follow-up):
  - Alpha-GPC use was linked to higher risk of total stroke (adjusted HR 1.43, 95% CI 1.41–1.46; the paper's summary says "46% higher"), ischemic stroke (1.34, 1.31–1.37) and hemorrhagic stroke (1.37, 1.29–1.46).
  - The risk rose with dose.
  - Proposed mechanism: gut bacteria turn alpha-GPC into TMA, which the liver converts to TMAO.
  - Sources: [JAMA Network Open 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2786547); [PMC8613599](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8613599/) [S]
- TMAO is being studied as a risk marker after ischemic stroke (BIOSIGNAL cohort) — [PMC12417456](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12417456/) [S; results not retrieved]
- EU status: novel food.
  - Alpha-GPC was not consumed to a significant degree before May 1997, so it counts as a novel food. This is the Swiss BLV classification of synthetic alpha-GPC; the EU also held a consultation on its status — [BLV classification PDF](https://www.blv.admin.ch/dam/blv/en/dokumente/lebensmittel-und-ernaehrung/rechts-und-vollzugsgrundlagen/lebensmittelrecht2017/novel-food/einstufung_synthetisches_alpha_gpc.pdf.download.pdf/Classification%20of%20synthetic%20L-alphaglycerylphosphorylcholine%20%28Alpha-GPC%29%20as%20novel%20food.pdf); [EU consultation document](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/4b990c14-4eeb-4166-99ef-4e0053ba1e3e_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_l-alpha_gpc.pdf) [S]
  - Chemi S.p.A. filed an EU novel-food application on 30 June 2023 for alpha-GPC from soya lecithin in food supplements, excluding infants and young children — [EU application summary](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/f0fcb3fa-4876-4558-934f-babe73399976_en?filename=novel-food_sum_ongoing-app_2023-12770.pdf&prefLang=pt) [S]
  - EFSA NDA opinion (EFSA Journal, 12 May 2026): proposed use in food supplements at up to 203.7 mg/day (82.5 mg/day choline) for everyone over 3 years, including pregnant and breastfeeding women. EFSA found that the product's identity, production process, composition and specifications raise no safety concerns — [EFSA Journal 2026](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10008) [S; EFSA's overall conclusion and any Commission authorisation not retrieved]

#### Ginkgo biloba
- Laws et al. 2012 (Human Psychopharmacology; meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials in healthy people):
  - Memory: 13 trials, 1,132 people. Executive function: 7 trials, 534 people. Attention: 8 trials, 910 people.
  - Effect sizes were not significant and close to zero. The young-adult group showed no significant effect on any measure.
  - Results did not depend on age, trial length, daily or total dose, or sample size.
  - Sources: [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/hup.2259); [PubMed 23001963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23001963/) [S; OLD, but no newer contrary meta-analysis found in healthy people]
- Safety in Germany:
  - The AkdÄ (Drug Commission of the German Medical Association) reported 185 adverse-reaction reports for Ginkgo biloba extracts in the joint BfArM/AkdÄ database, 20 of them for clotting disorders. Its headline warns: "Cave Kombination mit Gerinnungshemmern" (beware combining with anticoagulants).
  - Ginkgo promotes breakdown of blood clots and blocks platelet-activating factor. It may interact with anticoagulants, antiplatelet drugs, NSAIDs, antiepileptics and drugs with a narrow therapeutic window.
  - Sources: [AkdÄ](https://www.akdae.de/arzneimittelsicherheit/bekanntgaben/newsdetail/blutungen-unter-der-gabe-von-ginkgo-biloba-extrakten-cave-kombination-mit-gerinnungshemmern-aus-der-uaw-datenbank); [vitalstoff-lexikon](https://www.vitalstoff-lexikon.de/Weitere-Vitalstoffe/Ginkgo-biloba/Interaktionen) [S; AkdÄ date not retrieved]
- Pharmacy-only ginkgo medicines in Germany are standardised to 5 ppm ginkgolic acids or less. Many teas and supplements are not standardised and can contain much more, and their contents are not officially checked for efficacy or safety — [nordicoil blog](https://www.nordicoil.de/blogs/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/ginkgo-wechselwirkungen-blutverduenner-ssris) [S; WEAK]; [Apotheken Umschau](https://www.apotheken-umschau.de/medikamente/heilpflanzen/ginkgo-als-arzneimittel-wirkung-und-einnahme-736221.html) [S]

#### Panax ginseng
- Phytotherapy Research 2024 (systematic review and meta-analysis): ginseng had a significant effect on memory, especially at high doses; more high-quality studies are needed — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.8359); [PubMed 39474788](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39474788/) [S; effect sizes and doses not retrieved]
- ADDF review:
  - Acute doses of 100–400 mg improved attention and working memory in healthy adults.
  - 200 mg/day improved working memory after 8 weeks and mental arithmetic after 12 weeks, but not attention or concentration.
  - A meta-analysis of five double-blind 8–12-week trials found some improvement that was "not convincing or consistent".
  - Small samples and short follow-ups limit the evidence.
  - Sources: [ADDF rating](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/cognitive-vitality/ratings/panax-ginseng); [ADDF PDF](https://www.alzdiscovery.org/uploads/cognitive_vitality_media/Panax-ginseng-Cognitive-Vitality-For-Researchers.pdf) [S]
- A 2025 meta-analysis of MMSE and ADAS-Cog scores in clinical populations exists — [Karger CMR 2025](https://karger.com/cmr/article/32/4/283/931946/Cognitive-Benefits-of-Ginseng-A-Systematic-Review) [S; results not retrieved]

#### Rhodiola rosea
- Ishaque et al. 2012 (BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine; systematic review): 11 studies (10 randomized, 1 controlled non-randomized). Five looked at mental fatigue, and 3 of those 5 were positive. Outcomes were measured too inconsistently for a meta-analysis, every study had a high risk of bias or reporting flaws, and the evidence is contradictory — [BMC CAM 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1472-6882-12-70) [S; OLD]
- In a randomized trial in nursing students, results favoured placebo for fatigue at day 42 — [PMC4182456](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4182456/) [S]
- EMA/HMPC herbal monograph (2011/2012): "traditional use" as an adaptogen for temporary relief of stress symptoms such as fatigue, exhaustion and weakness. The preparation is a dry extract (drug-extract ratio 1.5–5:1, 67–70% ethanol) — [EMA assessment report](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/draft-assessment-report-rhodiola-rosea-first-version_en.pdf); [monograph copy](https://www.e-lactancia.org/media/papers/Rhodiola-DS-EMA2012.pdf) [S; OLD. "Traditional use" rests on long-standing use, not proof of efficacy]
- Health Canada monograph: at most 400 mg extract per day (equivalent to 2 g root) and 200 mg per single dose — [Health Canada](https://webprod.hc-sc.gc.ca/nhpid-bdipsn/dbImages/mono_rhodiola--rhodiola-rosea_english.pdf) [S; the combined search summary mixed Health Canada and EMA, so this attribution is uncertain]
- A 2022 review reports "encouraging clinical evidence" for stress-related symptoms — [PMC9228580](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9228580/) [S; details not retrieved]

#### Lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus)
- Docherty et al. 2023 (Nutrients; randomized parallel-group trial): 41 people aged 18–45 took 1.8 g/day. After a single dose, Stroop performance was faster at 60 min. After 28 days there was a trend towards lower subjective stress. Nothing else changed — [MDPI 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/22/4842) [S]
- Frontiers in Nutrition 2025 (randomized crossover trial): 18 people aged 18–35 took a single 3 g dose of a 10:1 fruiting-body extract. There was no effect on overall cognition or mood scores; pegboard (manual dexterity) performance improved at 90 min — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1405796/full) [S]
- EU status: the fruiting body and its water-extract powder are not novel (consumed before 15 May 1997). Dried mycelium powder is novel and needs authorisation — [functionalmushrooms.eu (2026)](https://functionalmushrooms.eu/novel-food-and-functional-mushrooms/) [S; WEAK commercial source, verify in the EU catalogue]

#### Cocoa flavanols
- COSMOS-Web (Brickman et al., PNAS 2023):
  - 3,562 older adults took 500 mg cocoa flavanols/day or placebo for 3 years.
  - The primary outcome (a hippocampal memory test, ModRey) did not change overall. It did improve in subgroups with poorer diet quality and low habitual flavanol intake.
  - A rise in a urinary flavanol marker correlated with memory improvement after 1 year.
  - Sources: [PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2216932120); [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/therapeutics/cocoa-flavanols) [S]
- The COSMOS clinic subcohort (AJCN 2024) also tested cocoa extract on cognitive function — [AJCN 2024](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2823%2966275-6/fulltext) [S; my existing knowledge (PK) is that it found no significant benefit over 2 years; verify]

#### B vitamins
- Nutrition Reviews 2025 (systematic review and meta-analysis in older adults): high-certainty evidence that B6, folate (B9) or B12 gives a very small benefit to overall cognitive function — [Nutrition Reviews 2025](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/nutrit/nuaf155/8251946); [PubMed 40966571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40966571/) [S]
- A 2021 meta-analysis: B vitamins and folate clearly lowered homocysteine but did not significantly prevent or slow cognitive decline compared with placebo — [PMC8207668](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8207668/) [S]
- Another meta-analysis (17 trials, 5,275 people) found a small-to-moderate improvement in overall cognition, with very low certainty — [search summary; probably Geriatric Nursing 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0197457223000526) [S; attribution uncertain]

### Inferences

**Evidence grades for healthy young adults**, synthesised from the findings above. A = consistent trials and meta-analyses plus position stands; B = a meta-analytic signal with heterogeneity or population limits; C = small or inconsistent trials; D = null or insufficient evidence.

| Ingredient | Best evidence, healthy young adults | Doses studied | Key safety and legal flags | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Omega-3 | Mixed; benefit mainly when DHA intake is low | 1.16 g DHA/d (Stonehouse); ~2 g/d in dose-response analysis | AF risk above ~1 g/d, clearest in high cardiovascular-risk patients | C |
| Bacopa | Small memory/attention signals after 12 weeks or more, mainly middle-aged and older adults | 300 mg/d (55% bacosides) | Digestive side effects; EU status unverified | C |
| Citicoline | Few industry-funded trials, ages 35–85 | 250–500 mg/d | EU-authorised up to 500 mg/d; not for children | C |
| Alpha-GPC | No healthy-adult cognition evidence retrieved | EFSA assessed up to 203.7 mg/d | Observational stroke signal; novel food, authorisation unclear | D, avoid |
| Ginkgo | Null in 2012 meta-analysis | n/a | Bleeding interactions; ginkgolic acids in non-drug products | D, avoid |
| Panax ginseng | Small, inconsistent; acute working-memory effects in small trials | 100–400 mg | Safety not retrieved | C− |
| Rhodiola | Low-quality, contradictory evidence on mental fatigue | ≤200 mg per dose, ≤400 mg/d (Health Canada) | "Traditional use" only | C−/D |
| Lion's mane | Two tiny trials with isolated effects | 1.8–3 g | Mycelium is a novel food | D |
| Cocoa flavanols | Benefit only in older adults with poor diets | 500 mg flavanols/d | none found | D |
| B vitamins | Correcting a deficiency only | n/a | none found | n/a (test first) |

- Industry funding and hosting (citicoline) and multi-ingredient formulas (bacopa) weaken most positive signals in this group.
- For a trainee on a normal mixed diet, the likely effect of any of these beyond caffeine plus sleep is small to zero. Money is better spent on correcting a real deficiency, confirmed by a blood test.

### Gaps
- Kongkeaw 2014, the key Bacopa meta-analysis, was not retrieved. From memory [PK], it reported improvements in the Trail Making Test B and choice reaction time.
- Not verified this session:
  - The EU Novel Food Catalogue entries for Bacopa, Rhodiola, Panax ginseng and Hericium.
  - The EFSA-authorised omega-3 claims: DHA and normal brain function, believed to apply at 250 mg DHA/day.
  - EFSA's 2012 upper safe intake for EPA+DHA (believed to be 5 g/day).
- Not retrieved: ginseng safety and interactions (for example insomnia, warfarin, blood glucose); rhodiola safety and interactions; the acute effect of cocoa flavanols in young adults.
- No data were found on B vitamins in healthy young adults specifically, or on B12 status in vegans and athletes.
- No 2024–2026 trials were found for ginkgo in healthy young adults.

## 4. Risky or not recommended: nicotine products, modafinil and stimulant medications, racetams/noopept and other unapproved nootropics, methylene blue

### Takeaway
- **Nicotine:** has real acute effects on motor function, attention and memory, but it is addictive. Nicotine pouches cannot legally be sold in Germany (nicht verkehrsfähig), and WADA monitors nicotine in competition.
- **Modafinil:** improves performance on complex tasks in rested healthy people, but it is prescription-only in Germany and banned in competition. Methylphenidate is a controlled narcotic (BtM) and also banned in competition.
- **Racetams, noopept, phenibut and similar:** unapproved medicines. EU official medicines-control laboratories find them mainly on the illegal market, sold as "supplements".
- **Methylene blue:** not permitted as a food supplement in the EU. As an MAO-A inhibitor it can cause serotonin syndrome with SSRIs and SNRIs; the risk at low oral doses is debated. It can also cause hemolysis in people with G6PD deficiency.

### Cited Findings

#### Nicotine
- Heishman et al. 2010 (Psychopharmacology; meta-analysis of 41 double-blind, placebo-controlled lab studies from 1994–2008 in non-smokers or smokers abstinent for no more than 2 h):
  - Nicotine significantly improved fine motor performance, alerting and orienting attention, short-term episodic memory and working memory.
  - Because the design excluded relief of withdrawal, these effects "likely represent true performance enhancement".
  - Sources: [Psychopharmacology 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-010-1848-1); [PMC3151730](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3151730/); [Cognitive Effects of Nicotine: Recent Progress, PMC6018192](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6018192/) [S; OLD]
- Germany, legal status of nicotine pouches:
  - Court-confirmed expert opinions classify tobacco-free nicotine pouches as food, so EU food law applies and they cannot be legally sold.
  - Nicotine is not approved in the EU as a food, food ingredient, additive or flavouring, and food must not be harmful. Toxicological studies show that even moderate pouch use delivers nicotine doses linked to harmful health effects.
  - In March 2021 the Hamburg Administrative Court ruled that marketing tobacco-free nicotine pouches is prohibited until the legal dispute is finally decided. Other courts ruled similarly.
  - Sources: [hamburg.de](https://www.hamburg.de/politik-und-verwaltung/behoerden/bukea/hu/aktuelles/verkaufsverbot-nikotinbeutel-179948); [LGL Bayern](https://www.lgl.bayern.de/lebensmittel/technologien/funktionelle_lebensmittel/et_nikotin_portionsbeutel.htm) [S]
- Bavaria's environment ministry announced firm action against nicotine and tobacco pouches (press release 157/25, 2025) — [StMUV Bayern](https://www.stmuv.bayern.de/aktuell/presse/pressemitteilung.htm?PMNr=157%2F25) [S; title only]
- A vendor blog says the BVL treats nicotine as an unauthorised novel food ingredient, commercial sale is effectively prohibited, and possession for personal use by adults is generally not a criminal offence — [pouchspot.de](https://pouchspot.de/blogs/journal/nikotinbeutel-deutschland-rechtslage-2026) [S; WEAK: pouch vendor]
- WADA's 2026 Monitoring Program covers nicotine and caffeine in competition only. They are monitored, not prohibited — [USADA 2026 advisory](https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/2026-wada-prohibited-list/); [WADA 2026 list](https://www.wada-ama.org/en/news/wada-publishes-2026-prohibited-list) [S]

#### Modafinil, methylphenidate and analogues
- Battleday & Brem 2015 (European Neuropsychopharmacology; systematic review of healthy people who were not sleep-deprived):
  - Modafinil gave significant cognitive benefits on a subset of tasks. The longer and more complex the task, the more consistent the benefit.
  - It improved decision-making and planning, but did not change working memory or mental flexibility.
  - 70% of studies that measured mood and side effects found little effect.
  - Sources: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924977X15002497); [Oxford University news, August 2015](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2015-08-20-review-%E2%80%98smart-drug%E2%80%99-shows-modafinil-does-enhance-cognition) [S; OLD]
- Germany: modafinil is prescription-only. It was removed from the Narcotics Act (BtMG) in 2008. Methylphenidate is a narcotic subject to BtM prescription rules — [drugcom.de Modafinil](https://www.drugcom.de/drogenlexikon/buchstabe-m/modafinil/); [Pharmazeutische Zeitung 2017](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/ausgabe-042017/was-ist-erlaubt-was-verboten/) [S]
- Anti-doping: methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine and modafinil are all prohibited in competition. A 2017 source adds that modafinil was not classed as a doping substance under German law at that time, even though WADA bans it — [Gemeinsam gegen Doping](https://www.gemeinsam-gegen-doping.de/was-ist-doping/verbotene-substanzen-und-methoden); [DSHS Köln doping lexicon: Methylphenidat](https://www.dshs-koeln.de/institut-fuer-biochemie/doping-substanzen/doping-lexikon/m/methylphenidat-ritalinr/); [PZ 2017](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/ausgabe-042017/was-ist-erlaubt-was-verboten/) [S; the German-law point is OLD, 2017, and may have changed]
- WADA 2026: flmodafinil and fladrafinil, unapproved potent analogues of modafinil and adrafinil found in "research only" and supplement products, were added to category S6.A (non-specified stimulants) — [USADA 2026 advisory](https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/2026-wada-prohibited-list/); [NADA 2026 changes](https://www.nada.de/en/medicine/prohibited-list/important-changes-to-the-2025-prohibited-list) [S]
- Related sources, titles only: a lawyer's article on modafinil ordered without prescription being held at customs — [anwalt.de](https://www.anwalt.de/rechtstipps/modafinil-ohne-rezept-bestellt-warum-die-sendung-beim-zoll-haengen-bleibt-276519.html); the Bavarian authority's consumer page "Neuroenhancement: Doping für das Gehirn" — [LGL Bayern](https://www.lgl.bayern.de/gesundheit/arzneimittel/warnungen_verbraucherinformationen/verbraucherinformationen/neuroenhancement.htm) [S; content not retrieved]

#### Racetams, noopept, phenibut and other unapproved nootropics
- Market-surveillance study by 12 Official Medicines Control Laboratories (OMCL network, 2025; Europe and Australia):
  - 159 samples collected from January 2020 to September 2024. 49% were sold or presented as dietary supplements and 32% as medicines; 69% came from the illegal market.
  - Noopept, phenylpiracetam and phenibut, which are prescription-only or unauthorised drugs in Russia, were found in pharmacological amounts, some as bulk raw material.
  - Phenibut is not authorised in the EU or Australia; products contained 81–250 mg per unit.
  - Sources: [PMC12193813](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12193813/); [EDQM summary report, June 2025](https://www.edqm.eu/documents/52006/628153/Summary%20Report%20MSSIP007%20Nootropics%20June%202025.pdf/6dd2098b-1fcb-0513-1e2d-c2c42f5f1a04?t=1749802811680) [S]
- Jędrejko et al. 2023 (Drug Testing and Analysis; review): according to the European Commission, many popular nootropics, including phenibut and other unapproved drugs, are unauthorised ingredients in food and supplements, and some could act as doping agents — [Wiley](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dta.3529) [S]
- A 2024 review documents brain-health supplements (cognition, mood, sleep) adulterated with undeclared pharmaceutical drugs — [PMC10969376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10969376/) [S; details not retrieved]
- Germany [CONTESTED, WEAK sources]: one source says noopept is not approved as a medicine but is not covered by the BtMG or the NpSG (New Psychoactive Substances Act); another calls it prescription-only. It can apparently be sold as a "research chemical" not intended for consumption — [noodoc](https://noodoc.com/noopept/); [supplement-bewertung](https://supplement-bewertung.com/noopept/) [S; WEAK]
- Piracetam is known as an anti-dementia drug, and some nootropics are approved medicines in Germany (listed in the Rote Liste) — [de.wikipedia: Nootropikum](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nootropikum) [S]

#### Methylene blue
- Mechanism and interactions: methylene blue is a potent MAO-A inhibitor. Combined with SSRIs, SNRIs or other serotonergic drugs it has caused serotonin syndrome, including fatal cases — [APSF](https://www.apsf.org/article/methylene-blue-and-the-risk-of-serotonin-toxicity/); [Ramsay et al. 2007, PMC2078225](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2078225/); [Psychopharmacology Institute, November 2025](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/publication/serotonin-syndrome-mechanisms-diagnosis-high-risk-interactions-and-management/) [S]
- Methylene blue is increasingly used for "biohacking" without telling doctors, and it is increasingly the "hidden" serotonergic agent in severe serotonin-syndrome cases — [Psychopharmacology Institute, November 2025](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/publication/serotonin-syndrome-mechanisms-diagnosis-high-risk-interactions-and-management/) [S]
- A 2025 cross-sectional study looked at how many patients given methylene blue during surgery were also taking serotonergic drugs — [PMC12017462](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12017462/) [S; details not retrieved]
- [CONTESTED] An opinion piece (April 2026) says 50 of the 51 published serotonin-syndrome cases involved high intravenous doses and none involved oral doses of 2–10 mg. Pharmacists, electronic-record alerts and training materials nevertheless treat any methylene blue + SSRI combination as contraindicated. The piece also notes that no formal data exist on adverse effects of compounded oral methylene blue — [KevinMD, April 2026](https://kevinmd.com/2026/04/51-cases-that-reframe-methylene-blue-serotonin-syndrome.html) [S; opinion, not peer-reviewed]
- EU and Germany: methylene blue is **not** authorised as a food supplement in the EU. Consumers obtain it indirectly, for example as a textile dye, without the quality controls that apply to supplements. There is no scientific basis for claimed performance benefits, and experts strongly advise against taking it. The risks are serotonin syndrome, hemolysis in G6PD deficiency and questionable product quality — [Pharmazeutische Zeitung, "Methylenblau-Hype im Check"](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/der-methylenblau-hype-im-check-150043/); [DAZ, 11 September 2024](https://www.deutsche-apotheker-zeitung.de/news/artikel/2024/09/11/was-hinter-dem-social-media-trend-um-methylenblau-steckt); [Medscape DE](https://deutsch.medscape.com/artikelansicht/4914807); [t-online](https://www.t-online.de/gesundheit/aktuelles/id_100620328/methylenblau-experten-warnen-vor-blaue-zunge-trend.html) [S; these came from one combined German search summary, so which statement comes from which article is uncertain]

### Inferences
- For a German trainee who competes, any "nootropic" beyond caffeine carries both a legal risk and an anti-doping risk:
  - Modafinil and methylphenidate are banned in competition.
  - The new modafinil analogues are banned and appear in "supplements".
  - Illegal-market nootropics may contain undeclared drugs.
- Nicotine: Heishman's design deliberately separated true enhancement from withdrawal relief in dependent smokers. That implies regular users become dependent, and the net benefit then shrinks, as with caffeine. On top of that, pouches cannot be legally sold in Germany.
- Methylene blue: whatever the low-dose debate, anyone on an SSRI or SNRI (a common medication in young adults) should treat it as contraindicated. It is also not a legal food supplement in the EU.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session:
  - Piracetam's exact prescription status in Germany (believed to be prescription-only).
  - Any EMA restriction of modafinil to narcolepsy (believed to be from about 2010).
  - Methylene blue's approved EU medicinal indication (believed to be methemoglobinemia).
- Not retrieved: current German anti-doping law (AntiDopG) coverage of modafinil; the 2017 statement may be out of date.
- Not retrieved: the planned EU Tobacco Products Directive revision ("TPD3") and its effect on pouch legality from 2026 onward.
- Not retrieved: data on dependence liability of nicotine pouches in non-smokers, and on cardiovascular effects in athletes.

## 5. Practical protocols: timing relative to training and work, stacking, cycling, interactions

### Takeaway
- **Timing:** take caffeine 30–60 min before demanding work or training, with the effect lasting into the 2nd hour. Stop caffeine about 9 h (about 100 mg) to 13 h (about 200 mg or more) before bed. Creatine timing does not matter for daily use; single high doses peak after about 4 h. Bacopa, citicoline and ginseng need 8–12 weeks or more.
- **Stacking:** evidence exists only for caffeine + L-theanine and citicoline + caffeine, and synergy in athletes is doubtful.
- **Cycling:** no trials found.
- **Key interactions:** ginkgo with anticoagulants or NSAIDs; methylene blue with SSRIs or SNRIs; high-dose omega-3 and atrial fibrillation; caffeine and sleep.
- **Athletes in Germany:** use Kölner Liste products only.

### Cited Findings

**Timing**
- Caffeine: 60 min before exercise is the most common protocol — [ISSN 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4). For sleep, about 107 mg at least 8.8 h and about 217.5 mg at least 13.2 h before bedtime — [Gardiner 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079223000205). EFSA considers 200 mg taken less than 2 h before intense exercise safe under normal environmental conditions — [EFSA 2015](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4102) [S]
- Caffeine + L-theanine: effects on attention appear in the 1st and 2nd hour, strongest in the 2nd — [Payne 2025](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/83/10/1873/8123998); [Camfield 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/nure.12120) [S]
- Creatine single high dose: peak effect about 4 h after intake, lasting up to 9 h (0.35 g/kg) — [Gordji-Nejad 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9). Effects were measured at 3, 5.5 and 7.5 h (0.2 g/kg) — [Gordji-Nejad 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/8/1192) [S]

**Stacking**
- Caffeine + L-theanine: small-to-moderate attention gains in pooled data — [Payne 2025](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/83/10/1873/8123998). ### Inferences
**A sensible, low-risk "cognition stack" for a German trainee.** This is my synthesis, not medical advice. Check it with a doctor if the trainee takes any medication, has a heart condition, or is pregnant.

1. **Foundation (no supplement):** sleep. Caffeine's benefit is largest when alertness is low, but caffeine itself erodes sleep, and sleep loss is the problem most of these supplements try to patch.
2. **Caffeine (grade A):**
   - 1–3 mg/kg (about 75–200 mg), taken 30–60 min before a focus block or training. Maximum 200 mg per dose and 400 mg/day.
   - Keep daily baseline use moderate. Use higher doses (3 mg/kg or more) for competitions, exams and sleep-deprived days.
   - Last caffeine about 9 h (about 100 mg) to 13 h (about 200 mg) before bed. Avoid late pre-workout products before evening sessions.
3. **Optional L-theanine (grade B/C):** 100–200 mg together with caffeine (roughly 1:1 to 2:1) if caffeine causes tension. Expect small effects on attention and mood in the 1st and 2nd hour, and no sport-performance synergy.
4. **Creatine monohydrate (grade B for training; C for cognition):**
   - 3–5 g/day, any time of day. Vegetarians and vegans are the most plausible cognitive responders.
   - Acute 0.2–0.35 g/kg "sleep-loss rescue" doses are experimental (two small German trials). Do not use them routinely until replicated, and test gut tolerance first.
   - Products must not advertise cognitive benefits (EFSA rejected the claim in 2024).
5. **Omega-3 only if fish intake is low (grade C):** a moderate dose of about 1 g/day EPA+DHA or less is enough. Higher doses bring an atrial fibrillation signal without a cognitive benefit proven in young adults.
6. **Test before supplementing:** B12 (and other nutrients) if vegan or vegetarian or if symptoms suggest a deficiency. B vitamins only help when a deficiency exists.
7. **Optional, low expectations:** citicoline 250–500 mg/day (EU-legal up to 500 mg; evidence mostly in people over 35), or bacopa 300 mg/day with food for at least 12 weeks (digestive side effects). Trial one at a time for 8–12 weeks and keep a log, since effects in the young are small.
8. **Avoid:**
   - Alpha-GPC: novel-food status unresolved and a stroke signal.
   - Ginkgo: no effect in healthy people and bleeding interactions.
   - Noopept, phenylpiracetam and phenibut: unapproved, sold on the illegal market.
   - Methylene blue: not a permitted supplement; serotonin syndrome risk with SSRIs/SNRIs; G6PD hemolysis.
   - Nicotine pouches: not legally sold in Germany; addictive.
   - Modafinil and methylphenidate without a prescription: illegal to obtain that way, and banned in competition.
9. **Athletes in tested sport:** single-ingredient products from the Kölner Liste only (checkable in the NADA app). Avoid multi-ingredient "nootropic" blends, since they combine weak evidence with higher contamination and analogue risk.
10. **Cycling:** no evidence either way. For caffeine, the withdrawal-reversal data argue for intermittent or strategic use rather than a formal "cycle". For creatine there is no rationale for cycling in the evidence retrieved here.

### Gaps
- No trials were found on cycling or on caffeine "resets" and cognitive outcomes.
- No randomized trials were found of multi-component stacks (for example caffeine + theanine + creatine) in athletes or trainees.
- Not researched: vitamin D, iron/ferritin and other deficiency-related cognitive symptoms common in athletes. These are outside this scope but relevant for the "test first" step.
- Not retrieved: interactions of ginseng and rhodiola with medications, and caffeine interactions with medications such as hormonal contraceptives or fluvoxamine.
- Not retrieved: caffeine use in hot environments. EFSA's exercise statement is limited to "normal environmental conditions".
